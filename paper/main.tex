\begin{abstract}
When two language models achieve the same score on a benchmark, are they
solving problems the same way? We show they are not: identical accuracy can
be reached through genuine computation or surface pattern recognition, and
the two strategies only become distinguishable beyond the accuracy number.

We apply three independent diagnostic tests---surface rename, cross-session
plan-vs-execution consistency, and training-data proximity---to 219 reasoning
problems across arithmetic, planning, and algorithmic optimization, running
five models (including the reasoning-trained o4-mini) for approximately 20{,}000
API calls. Per-instance labels are issued only when all three probes converge,
preventing any single confound from driving a conclusion.

Three findings stand out. First, the same block-rename operation
\emph{improves} two models, \emph{destroys} one, and leaves two untouched---on
identical problems. Second, weighted interval scheduling collapses every model
to zero under entity rename despite near-perfect canonical accuracy, isolating
algorithmic depth as a robustness axis orthogonal to benchmark score. Third,
models accept injected wrong intermediate states at high rates yet still recover
the correct final answer roughly half the time, showing that compliance and
derailment are empirically separable.

None of these patterns is visible from benchmark accuracy alone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Imagine two students score 80\% on the same exam. You rename every
person in every word problem; numbers and structure are unchanged.
Student A still scores 80\%; Student B drops to 0\%. Who actually
understood the material? This is, roughly, what we observe in
frontier LLMs: two models can reach identical benchmark accuracy via
fundamentally different strategies, and standard accuracy reporting
cannot tell them apart.

\paragraph{The surprising finding.}
On shortest-path planning, Claude Sonnet~4 scores 64.7\% vs.\
GPT-4o's 41.2\%, then drops to 0.0\% under pure entity renaming
while GPT-4o retains 26.5\%. On coin-change the direction reverses.
This is not a two-model anecdote: across Claude, GPT-4o, Gemini, and
Llama, robustness ordering is family-dependent and cannot be
inferred from canonical accuracy.

\paragraph{Why one test is not enough.}
A drop under renaming alone admits mundane explanations
(tokenization, residual difficulty, noise). Our approach is
convergence: three independent tests with non-overlapping
assumptions. Prior
work~\citep{mirzadeh2024gsm,wu2024reasoning,wang2024rupbench} reports
group-level drops; we diagnose individual instances by asking
whether all three signals agree.

\paragraph{What is genuinely new.}
Three things distinguish this work from prior studies of LLM
robustness (see Table~\ref{tab:novelty}). First, \emph{per-problem
diagnosis}: rather than reporting an average accuracy drop across a
group of problems \citep{mirzadeh2024gsm,huang2025mathperturb}, we
issue a label for \emph{each individual problem} only when three
independent signals (surface rename, plan-execution consistency,
training-exposure proximity) converge. Second, an \emph{injection
dissociation}: when we pause a model mid-solve and feed it a wrong
intermediate answer, models accept it in 88--93\% of cases yet still
arrive at the correct final answer $\sim$52\% of the time. Accepting
a false intermediate state and being derailed by it are empirically
separable. Third, a \emph{built-in training-exposure control}: three
algorithmic subtypes share the same DP algorithm but differ
dramatically in public-corpus prevalence; the low-exposure subtype
collapses under name change across every model, while the
structurally identical high-exposure subtype partially survives for
some.

\paragraph{The three tests.}
We apply three independent diagnostics to every problem
(Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}; full pipeline in Appendix~\ref{app:pipeline}).
\textit{Test~1 (surface).} Six structure-preserving variants per
problem; the diagnostic variant $W_3$ renames every identifier to
novel meaningless tokens.
\textit{Test~2 (execution).} A two-session protocol: declare the
complete strategy in session A, then execute step-by-step in a
session-isolated loop with no Phase-1 memory; CCI compares
declaration to execution, and a separate injection arm pauses the
solve to feed a deliberately wrong intermediate state.
\textit{Test~3 (exposure).} Infini-gram overlap with The
Pile/DCLM~\citep{liu2024infini}. Only when all three converge do we
label an instance as behaviour consistent with training-pattern
matching; numbers are re-derivable from per-instance verifier logs
in the audit bundle (Appendix~\ref{app:audit}).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Surface sensitivity.}
\citet{mirzadeh2024gsm} (GSM-Symbolic) show arithmetic accuracy drops
under template-variable perturbation in a single domain at the
\emph{group-mean} level.
\citet{wu2024reasoning} extend this across 11 counterfactual tasks
(again group-level).
\citet{wang2024rupbench} (RUPBench) apply nine perturbation types over
15 reasoning datasets.
\citet{li2024gsm} (GSM-Plus) generate diverse variants of GSM8K and
find consistent accuracy drops.
This paper differs from the surface-sensitivity line in three
concrete ways that no prior work combines: \textbf{(i)} three
\emph{orthogonal} probes (surface rename, cross-session execution
consistency, training-exposure proximity) coupled \emph{per
individual instance}, allowing a convergence label that group-level
means cannot produce; \textbf{(ii)} a built-in training-exposure
gradient inside one fixed algorithm family (DP across CC/SP/WIS)
that holds the algorithm constant while varying public-corpus
prevalence by orders of magnitude; \textbf{(iii)} identification of
matched-canonical (model, subtype) pairs that dissociate in
\emph{opposite} directions on the same input---a pattern invisible
to aggregate robustness scores.

\paragraph{CoT faithfulness.}
\citet{turpin2023language} show CoT explanations can misrepresent the
causes of model predictions.
\citet{lanham2023measuring} measure faithfulness via CoT interventions,
finding larger models often condition less on stated reasoning.
\citet{saparov2023language} formally analyze CoT on synthetic tasks.
\citet{lightman2023let} supervise step-level correctness during
training.
\citet{tutek2025measuring} measure \emph{parametric} faithfulness by
unlearning individual reasoning steps and observing the effect on
predictions (EMNLP 2025 Outstanding Paper).
Our cross-session CCI is qualitatively different from all of these:
prior faithfulness work intervenes \emph{within} a single trace
(perturb-and-rerun, truncate-and-resample, unlearn-and-repredict,
or step-level verification), so the model retains its declaration in
context. CCI compares a declaration made in session~A against a
per-step execution in session~B with \emph{no shared context, no chat
history, and a fresh API call per step}; the metric is the fraction
of declared numeric values that match executed numeric values exactly
($\varepsilon{=}0.01$). It therefore measures behavioural
plan-execution consistency under hard session isolation, not
within-trace faithfulness.

\paragraph{Training-data contamination.}
\citet{razeghi2022impact} show pretraining term frequency predicts
arithmetic accuracy.
\citet{golchin2023time} detect contamination via guided instruction
completion.
\citet{shi2024detecting} extend contamination analysis to reasoning
benchmarks.
For closed-weight models we treat Infini-gram overlap as a
\emph{public-corpus proximity proxy}; we discuss its limitations and
partial validation via the Llama open-weight model in
Section~\ref{subsec:contam}.

\paragraph{LLM planning and execution.}
\citet{valmeekam2023planning} demonstrate systematic Blocksworld
planning failure.
We extend PlanBench with a cross-session execution probe and a
false-state injection probe, testing whether models that accept an
injected error maintain correct final-answer generation.

\paragraph{Concurrent work on reasoning-model robustness.}
\citet{vonrecum2026reasoning} study how reasoning-trained LLMs respond
to seven interventions (benign, neutral, adversarial) applied to
their own CoT tokens at fixed timesteps across MATH, SCIENCE, and
LOGIC tasks, finding that model size and intervention timing determine
recovery. Our setup is mechanistically distinct in two ways: (i) we
intervene on externally provided \emph{state} (a wrong intermediate
value injected into a fresh session), not on the model's own generated
tokens, so the model has no CoT trace to recover from; (ii) our
cross-session isolation means the model receives \emph{no prior
reasoning context} whatsoever at each injection step. The two designs
are therefore complementary: von Recum et al.\ measure within-trace
self-correction, while our Probe-2B measures state-compliance and
correctness independence.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We test five AI models---four frontier chat models and the
reasoning-trained o4-mini---on three types of reasoning problems,
each presented in up to seven different surface forms.
Every problem has a verified correct answer, and every surface variant
preserves that correct answer exactly---only the presentation changes.
The core question: does the model still get it right?

\paragraph{Problem families.}
Three reasoning domains, chosen to dissociate three confounds that
would otherwise undermine the probes individually:
\textbf{(i)} GSM (arithmetic) calibrates the probes against a
benchmark with documented contamination structure but very different
algorithmic style;
\textbf{(ii)} BW (planning) exposes the format-vs-policy confound
(``did the model actually fail, or did the parser?'') and provides
the matched standard/mystery contrast that directly probes lexical
content while holding planning structure fixed;
\textbf{(iii)} ALGO (algorithmic optimisation) is the only family
where we can hold the underlying algorithm fixed (DP) while varying
training-text exposure across three orders of magnitude---this
is what makes the same-algorithm vs.\ different-exposure control in
Section~\ref{subsec:contam} possible at all.

\textit{Arithmetic word problems (GSM)}: 44 problems drawn from the
GSM8K benchmark~\citep{cobbe2021training}, selected so that some have
high overlap with public training text and others have low overlap.
Standard multi-step arithmetic: ``Alice has 12 apples...''

\textit{Block-stacking planning (BW)}: 65 planning problems from
PlanBench~\citep{valmeekam2023planbench}.
The model must figure out a sequence of moves to rearrange blocks
from a starting configuration to a goal configuration.
Includes standard, extended, and ``Mystery'' variants where objects
have unusual names from the start.

\textit{Algorithmic optimization (ALGO)}: 110 problems across three
subtypes.
All three require the same algorithmic technique (dynamic programming)
under the hood, but they differ drastically in training-data exposure:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1em,itemsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{Coin change (CC)}: find the minimum number of coins
that sum to a target. Very common in programming tutorials online.
\item \textbf{Shortest path (SP)}: find the minimum-cost route
through a weighted graph. Moderately common.
\item \textbf{Weighted interval scheduling (WIS)}: select the
maximum-value set of non-overlapping time intervals. Rarely
phrased this way in public text.
\end{itemize}
\noindent This three-way comparison is our built-in training-exposure
control: same algorithm, very different levels of prior exposure.

\paragraph{Seven versions of every problem.}
For each canonical problem we create six variants, each preserving
the correct answer but changing the surface presentation:
\textbf{W1}---reworded in different sentences (paraphrase);
\textbf{W2}---reformatted (word problem to table, or prose to
edge list);
\textbf{W3}---\emph{all names replaced} with novel made-up tokens that
carry no meaning (entity rename; the critical diagnostic variant;
Appendix~\ref{app:variants});
\textbf{W4}---rewritten in formal mathematical notation;
\textbf{W5}---direction reversed (goal and start swapped, or
problem asked backwards), where applicable;
\textbf{W6}---same algorithm required, completely new numbers.
Every variant is automatically verified to have the same correct
answer as the original before any model sees it.

\paragraph{Models.}

\section{The Three Diagnostic Tests}
\label{sec:probes}

Each probe introduces an assumption the other two do not share:
$W_3$ drops could be tokenisation noise alone; low CCI could be
sampling variance alone; high proximity could be surface
co-occurrence rather than memorisation. We require convergence, so
no single confound can drive a label.

\paragraph{Test 1 (Surface robustness).}
$W_3$ retention $R_{W_3}{=}\mathrm{Acc}_{W_3}/\mathrm{Acc}_\mathrm{canon}$;
near $1.0$ means renaming had no effect, $0.0$ means full collapse.
The Vocabulary Robustness Index (VRI) extends this by comparing
$W_3$ to three vocabulary-preserving variants.

\paragraph{Test 2 (Cross-session consistency).}
Part~A: ask the model to write out its complete solution strategy
with all numeric step values in a fresh session; then execute the
solve in a separate session-isolated loop with no Phase-1 memory.
\textit{CCI} is the fraction of declared steps that match the
execution by direct numeric comparison ($\varepsilon{=}0.01$); no LM
judge.
Part~B: pause the model mid-solve and feed it a wrong intermediate
state. We measure injection compliance (does the model accept it?)
and post-injection final-answer accuracy.
\textit{TEP} quantifies how strongly the injection deflects
subsequent reasoning.

\paragraph{Test 3 (Training exposure).}
We compute Infini-gram overlap between each problem's text and The
Pile/DCLM. For closed-weight models this is a public-corpus
proximity \emph{proxy}, not a confirmed signal.

\paragraph{Convergence.}
An instance is labelled \emph{retrieval-consistent} only when all
three signals align (surface collapse + low CCI + high proximity)
and \emph{computation-consistent} only when all three reverse. Under
these strict rules $\sim$62\% of ALGO instances remain ambiguous; we
treat ambiguity as the honest outcome: forcing a label on ambiguous
instances would be statistically unjustifiable, and the 12
high-confidence labels serve as existence proofs that both
retrieval-consistent and computation-consistent behaviour occur
within the same model on the same problem family.

Figure~\ref{fig:robustness} situates all five models in the
accuracy--robustness landscape across families.
On GSM (Table~\ref{tab:gsm}, Figure~\ref{fig:robustness}), Claude,
GPT-4o, and Llama-8B sit within $2$pp of each other on canonical
accuracy ($0.825$--$0.841$); under renaming Claude retains 75\%

\emph{highest} on canonicals (91\%) yet retains only 52\% under
renaming --- high benchmark accuracy and high surface fragility can
co-occur.

\paragraph{The pattern is sharper on algorithmic problems.}
Shortest-path: Claude 64.7\% canonical ([0.48,0.79]) collapses to
0.0\% under renaming ([0.00,0.10]); GPT-4o 41.2\% ([0.26,0.58])
retains 26.5\% ([0.15,0.43]). The \emph{better} model on the
canonical is the \emph{worse} one under rename. Coin-change reverses
the direction: Claude 60\% retention ([0.31,0.83]), GPT-4o 0\%
([0.00,0.28]). Robustness ordering depends on the problem subtype.

\paragraph{Statistical confidence.}
The inversion replicates on matched-canonical subsets: SP ($n{=}34$)
Claude 0/34 vs.\ GPT-4o 9/34 (Fisher $p{=}0.0021$); CC ($n{=}10$)
Claude 6/10 vs.\ GPT-4o 0/10 ($p{=}0.0108$; exploratory, $n{=}10$).
Non-overlapping Wilson CIs on the full SP subtype are the stronger
argument; the within-model per-problem phi coefficients
($\phi_\text{GPT-4o}{=}{+}0.43$, Fisher $p{<}0.0001$;
$\phi_\text{o4-mini}{=}{+}0.53$, Fisher $p{=}0.03$;
Appendix~\ref{app:full_var}) provide the highest-$n$ evidence that
canonical-correct problems are also more likely to be $W_3$-correct.

\paragraph{Not a population-level law.}
At the model-rank level, more accurate models retain \emph{more}
canonical accuracy under $W_3$ on ALGO ($\rho{=}{+}0.90$, $p{=}0.04$,
$n{=}5$; directional only---does not survive Holm--Bonferroni); GSM
trends the same direction (n.s.) and BW is flat. The dissociation is
therefore a per-(model, subtype) phenomenon, not a universal inverse
axis; a population-level test is reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:population_test}.

\paragraph{What about the other variants?}
The full per-variant profile (Figure~\ref{fig:decay},
Appendix~\ref{app:full_var}) shows a robust asymmetry: $W_6$ (new
numbers, same algorithm) is at or above canonical for two of four
models on GSM (Claude $\pm 0$pp, Gemini $+4$pp; GPT-4o $-8$pp, Llama
$-8$pp), while $W_3$ (same numbers, new names) drops sharply for
three of four models. Changing numbers preserves accuracy; changing
names degrades it. That asymmetry is consistent with models relying
on specific vocabulary rather than mathematical structure.

\paragraph{Twenty-six universally fragile problems.}
Of 110 ALGO problems, 26 are universally fragile: every model that
solves them canonically scores zero on $W_3$ (12 SP, 11 WIS, 3 CC).
Per-model $W_3$-collapse rates range from Llama 100\% (6/6) to
o4-mini 46\% (12/26); per-problem collapse is \emph{not} predicted
by proximity for any single model (Mann--Whitney $p{>}0.13$).
Fragility is a property of the instance, not of exposure.

\paragraph{Algorithmic depth dominates absolute accuracy: WIS is universally fatal.}
The full $3\!\times\!7\!\times\!5$ subtype grid
(Appendix~\ref{app:full_var}, Figure~\ref{fig:subtype_grid}) reveals
three patterns family-level numbers hide: \textbf{(a)} every model
reaching $\ge\!95\%$ canonical on WIS drops to $0\%$ on $W_3$/$W_6$,
including o4-mini ($1.00\!\to\!0.00$); \textbf{(b)} shortest-path
$W_5$ (numeric scale perturbation) is a universal floor across the
suite ($\le\!4\%$); \textbf{(c)} o4-mini's $W_3$ retention varies
from $1.00$ (CC) to $0.87$ (SP) to $0.00$ (WIS) despite $1.00$
canonical everywhere. \emph{Algorithmic depth is an axis of
brittleness orthogonal to absolute accuracy.}

\paragraph{Claude and GPT-4o follow different reasoning strategies---CCI is a behavioral fingerprint, not a performance predictor.}
Comparing what each model declared in session~1 against what it did
in session~2, Claude's numeric steps match $\sim$23\% on average;
GPT-4o's $\sim$11\% (median $=0$ over 29/44 sessions). Yet GPT-4o
still answers correctly 70.5\% of the time. The consistency gap is
significant (paired Wilcoxon $W{=}396$, $p{=}0.0068$, $n{=}44$;
Table~\ref{tab:p2}; Appendix~\ref{app:p2protocol},
Figure~\ref{fig:cci}) but does not explain
accuracy: within Claude's 44 sessions, declaration-following has no
relationship with final correctness (Spearman $\rho{=}0.14$,
$p{=}0.38$). \textbf{Claude and GPT-4o are different \emph{kinds} of
reasoners, not different levels of the same kind: Claude consistent
and structured; GPT-4o flexible and path-independent. CCI is a
fingerprint, not a score.}

\paragraph{What ``$CCI{=}0$'' actually means---two separable behaviours.}
Decomposing GPT-4o's 29/44 zero-CCI sessions: $17/29$ have
$\mathrm{cci\_total}{=}0$ (no parser-extractable numeric steps
declared in Phase~1), and $12/29$ have $\mathrm{cci\_total}{>}0$ but
zero matches (declared then diverged). Final-answer correctness is
nearly identical in the two ($0.69$ vs.\ $0.73$, ns), reinforcing
that step declaration and final-answer production are largely
independent for GPT-4o. Cross-model zero-CCI rates: Gemini $10/44$
(23\%), Claude $14/44$ (32\%), Llama $24/44$ (55\%), GPT-4o $29/44$
(66\%).

\paragraph{What happens when we feed a model a wrong answer mid-solve?}
Over 61 mid-solve injections on ALGO problems, every model except
Gemini accepts the wrong intermediate state at very high rates---
Claude 88.5\%, GPT-4o 93.4\%, Llama 39.3\%, \emph{o4-mini 100\%}
(every injection followed). Gemini accepts \emph{none}
(format-ignores every injection). Despite near-universal acceptance,
Claude still answers correctly 52.5\% of the time (uninterrupted:
54.1\%), GPT-4o 50.8\% (uninterrupted: 55.7\%), and o4-mini 37.7\%
(uninterrupted: 40.9\%). Compliance and final correctness are
therefore \emph{empirically separable}---a finding that complements
\citet{vonrecum2026reasoning}'s within-trace recovery results by
showing that independence holds even when the model has no prior
reasoning context to recover from. Two non-exclusive accounts:
(i) the injection arrives \emph{after} the answer direction has been
implicitly determined, or (ii) models re-derive intermediates rather
than chain from previous outputs; behavioural data cannot distinguish
them. Notably the reasoning-trained o4-mini is the \emph{most}
compliant of any model: stronger reasoning training does not produce
selective rejection of injected wrong steps.

\paragraph{Plausibility of the injection does not matter: a five-model null result.}
With the implausible arm now run on \emph{all five} models on the same
61 adversarial problems, we can test the plausibility-detection
hypothesis directly with a paired Wilcoxon on
final-answer correctness (plausible vs.\ implausible).
\emph{No model} significantly distinguishes the two arms:
Claude $\Delta{=}0.0$pp ($p{=}1.00$),
GPT-4o $\Delta{=}{+}4.9$pp ($p{=}0.50$),
Llama $\Delta{=}{+}3.3$pp ($p{=}0.69$),
Gemini $\Delta{=}{-}3.3$pp ($p{=}0.64$),
o4-mini $\Delta{=}{+}4.9$pp ($p{=}0.48$)
(Appendix~\ref{app:p2protocol}, Figure~\ref{fig:implaus}).
At $n{=}61$ paired problems per model, a Fisher exact test has 80\%
power to detect effects $\ge$25pp (OR $\ge$ 3.0); the observed deltas
(0.0--4.9pp) are well below this threshold, supporting the null
interpretation. Models do \emph{not} selectively detect
implausibility; they recover at roughly their uninterrupted rate
either way.

\paragraph{Algorithm elicitation raises invocation, not accuracy: a five-model null.}
Prompting the model to name its algorithm before each step raises the
algorithm-invocation rate $10\!-\!50{\times}$ (e.g.\ $\le 1.7\%$
un-elicited $\to 55.2\%$ Claude, $52.5\%$ Gemini, $19.9\%$ Llama),
confirming the prompt works. Yet final-answer accuracy does
\emph{not} improve in any of the five models: Claude
$0.500{\to}0.459$ ($-4.1$pp), GPT-4o $0.500{\to}0.518$ ($+1.8$pp),
Gemini $0.300{\to}0.311$ ($+1.1$pp), Llama $0.218{\to}0.148$
($-7.0$pp), o4-mini $0.436{\to}0.418$ ($-1.8$pp). Mean
$\Delta{=}{-}1.6$pp; no individual $\Delta$ is significant (Fisher
$p{=}0.21$--$0.83$). This extends prior faithfulness
work~\citep{turpin2023language,lanham2023measuring} to
algorithm-specific declarative elicitation under interactive
execution---a setup where, if explicit algorithm knowledge causally
influenced computation, the effect should be maximal. Even the
reasoning-trained o4-mini does not benefit. The surface marker
``names the algorithm'' is decoupled from the latent computation that
produces the answer.

\paragraph{Training exposure predicts name-change fragility per problem.}

and points toward model-specific training distributions.

Higher training exposure correlates with more rigid plan reproduction

independent metrics, both selectively sensitive to training overlap,
both pointing at the same model.

non-reasoning models, but the conclusion is one-model-strong and we
flag it as a directional observation rather than a tested law.

\subsection{Blocksworld: when the measurement protocol itself breaks down}
\label{subsec:bw_p2}

Under our interactive PDDL step protocol, 84--100\% of BW Phase-2A
sessions abort before reliable CCI can be computed
(Claude 16\% complete; GPT-4o 0\%; Llama 2\%) ---
a \emph{structural protocol limit}, not a model-level CCI result.
The failure mode itself is informative
(Appendix~\ref{app:p2protocol}, Figure~\ref{fig:violations}):
Claude maintains the highest semantic
validity (0.68) and almost never repeats actions (rep.\ rate 0.00),
GPT-4o has the highest \emph{format} failure rate (the standard PDDL
parser misses 25.6 actions per session) but acceptable semantic
validity (0.58), and Llama collapses on both axes (semantic
validity 0.27, repetition rate 0.23, median first-illegal step 1).
The three models thus reach the same low CCI through three
qualitatively different breakdowns---a behavioural fingerprint that
canonical accuracy hides. We scope this section's contribution
accordingly: the BW Probe-2 result is a measurement-protocol finding
that is itself informative about how interactive PDDL evaluation
fails across frontier models.

A re-run with an NL-tolerant executor lifts standard-BW from 0/150
to 14/150 (Fisher $p{=}8.9{\times}10^{-5}$); Mystery-BW stays at
0/45. The standard$\to$mystery contrast aligns with the
\emph{mechanistic dissociation} we observe in the open-weight
Qwen-2.5-7B pilot: on all four residual-stream metrics (final rank,
depth-resolved rank, log-prob, and cosine similarity), $W_6$ surfaces
the canonical gold token in algorithmic ($22{,}472\to 156$) and
planning ($419\to 49$) families
(Appendix~\ref{app:mechanistic}, Figure~\ref{fig:mechanistic});
mystery-BW preserves the planning structure but obfuscates lexical
content, exactly the manipulation that should leave a retrieval
account behaviourally floored. Apparent Probe-1 anomalies
(e.g.\ GPT-4o $W_3{=}24.4\%$ vs.\ canonical $8.9\%$) sit inside
overlapping Wilson CIs and reflect the very low baseline, not a
robustness gain. Full BW protocol detail and abort taxonomy appear
in Appendix~\ref{app:p2protocol}; the mechanistic pilot in
Appendix~\ref{app:mechanistic} (including the arithmetic-family
reversal that the paper does \emph{not} claim is general).

\paragraph{Renaming Blocksworld blocks flips the sign of accuracy for some models and destroys it for others.}
Running the paired Wilcoxon between BW canonical and the
renamed-blocks variant $W_5$ on the same problem IDs (a within-model
test, $n{=}65$--$109$ per model depending on instance coverage), four
qualitatively different responses emerge
(Appendix~\ref{app:full_var}, Figure~\ref{fig:bw_inversion}).
For \textbf{Claude}, renaming
\emph{improves} BW accuracy from $0.422$ to $0.661$
($+23.9$pp, $p{=}1.0{\times}10^{-4}$); for \textbf{Gemini} from $0.385$
to $0.569$ ($+18.5$pp, $p{=}0.014$); for \textbf{Llama} renaming
\emph{destroys} BW accuracy from $0.321$ to $0.101$
($-22.0$pp, $p{<}10^{-4}$); \textbf{GPT-4o} and \textbf{o4-mini} are
unchanged ($\Delta{=}0$pp, n.s.). The opposite signs across
architectures are direct behavioural evidence that the canonical
Blocksworld surface form carries \emph{load-bearing} cues for Llama
(removing them collapses the model) but \emph{net-harmful} cues for
Claude and Gemini (removing them lifts the model toward genuine
planning). GPT-4o and o4-mini appear robust to either phrasing.
This effect is invisible in any single-model accuracy report; it
emerges only when the same instances are run paired across surface
forms and the test is paired Wilcoxon, not unpaired aggregate.

\subsection{When all three tests agree}
\label{subsec:convergence}

Across 440 algorithmic optimization problem-instances (110 problems
$\times$ 4 models), applying strict thresholds to all three tests:
8 instances show all three signals pointing toward training-pattern
matching (6 Claude + 2 Gemini, concentrated on shortest-path).
4 instances show all three signals pointing toward genuine computation
(3 GPT-4o + 1 Claude); 157 mixed; 271 (61.6\%) ambiguous. The high
ambiguity rate is the conservative outcome of requiring three
independent signals to align; the 12 high-confidence labels are
individually verifiable in the released raw data. For arithmetic
problems (Claude only, $n{=}44$), 35 converge toward genuine
computation and 9 are ambiguous.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Per-model failure signatures.}
The five models cluster into qualitatively different reasoners, not
levels of the same reasoner: Claude declares plans most reliably
(CCI $=0.231$) but plan fidelity does not predict correctness;
GPT-4o almost never executes its declaration (median CCI $=0$) yet
recovers; Gemini peaks on canonicals (91\%) but format-ignores every
injected state; Llama sits lowest overall; o4-mini solves canonicals
near-perfectly and complies with $100\%$ of injections. Triangulation
extends naturally to tool-using agents (declared tool plan vs.\
executed calls), RAG (cited vs.\ supporting passages), and
multi-agent coordination; subtype comparisons require an
exposure-floor control such as our WIS bank.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Benchmarks are not broken; treating a single accuracy number as the
complete picture of a model's reasoning is. Across 219 problems, six
surface variants, five models, and $\sim$20{,}000 API calls, three
independent tests reveal patterns accuracy alone cannot:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1em,topsep=0pt,itemsep=0pt,parsep=0pt]
\item Matched-benchmark models differ in $W_3$ robustness, and the
direction reverses across problem types.
\item Plan fidelity does not predict correctness: GPT-4o (median
CCI $=0$) is correct $\sim$70\% of the time.
\item Accepting a wrong intermediate at 88--93\% does not reliably
derail the final answer.
\item Same DP algorithm, different training exposure: WIS collapses
under $W_3$ for every model; CC partially survives.
\item BW block-rename \emph{improves} Claude ($+23.9$pp) and Gemini
($+18.5$pp), \emph{destroys} Llama ($-22.0$pp), and leaves
GPT-4o/o4-mini untouched.
\item Algorithm elicitation raises invocation rate $10\!-\!50\times$
yet improves accuracy in 0/5 models ($\Delta{=}{-}2.5$pp,
$p{=}0.20$).
\end{itemize}
\noindent Minimum actionable change: report canonical \emph{and}
$W_3$-rename accuracy with CIs---the gap is where the real
capability difference lives.

\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The findings reported here rest on a set of deliberate scoping
decisions; we summarise the most consequential ones and the
trade-offs they reflect.

\paragraph{Scope: structured reasoning, English, zero-shot at $T{=}0$.}
We fix the prompting regime to zero-shot CoT at temperature~0 across
all $\sim$20{,}000 calls because the scientific question is whether
matched canonical accuracy hides divergent strategies---a question
that is cleanest when prompt context is held constant. Few-shot
demonstrations are known to partially restore accuracy under
template perturbation~\citep{mirzadeh2024gsm}; we therefore present
our $W_3$ numbers as the zero-shot estimate, not as the upper bound
of model capability. Likewise, the three task families we cover
(arithmetic word problems, DP optimisation, planning) were chosen
to dissociate three confounds that would otherwise undermine the
probes individually (Section~\ref{sec:setup}); they are English
structured-reasoning tasks. We scope our claims accordingly to
\emph{structured reasoning} rather than over-claim generalisation to
free-text NLP tasks (NLI, QA, summarisation) or to non-English
prompts, both of which warrant their own studies.

\paragraph{Probe-3 is a public-corpus proximity proxy by design.}
Public training corpora are unknown for closed-weight models, so
\emph{any} contamination measure for those models is a proxy.
Rather than assert exposure, we use Infini-gram overlap on The
Pile/DCLM as an explicitly labelled \emph{proximity proxy} and
require it to converge with the other two probes before we apply a
``retrieval-consistent'' label (Section~\ref{subsec:convergence}).
The within-ALGO same-algorithm/different-exposure gradient
(CC$\to$SP$\to$WIS) is the cleaner internal control; WIS does carry
a residual difficulty co-variance, and a difficulty-matched WIS bank
(canonical accuracy $\sim$60--70\%) is the priority follow-up
experiment.

\paragraph{Blocksworld Probe-2: a measurement-protocol finding.}
The strict PDDL step protocol aborts at $84\!-\!100\%$ rates across
all three models we ran it on
(Section~\ref{subsec:bw_p2})---which is itself a
finding about how brittle interactive PDDL evaluation is, not a
property of one model. Once the protocol failure was established on
the existing pool, the responsible call was to canonicalise the
NL-tolerant rerun (which covers all five models) rather than spend
additional API budget on a protocol already documented as broken;
we therefore report \emph{n/a} on the strict variant for Gemini and
o4-mini. A parsing-tolerant interactive protocol may expose
additional BW-specific failure modes and is the natural follow-up.

\paragraph{Reasoning-trained models: one provider in depth, others as next step.}
The reasoning-trained suite is represented by o4-mini, which we ran
on every probe at full coverage. This was a depth-vs-breadth
trade-off: running a second reasoning-trained provider
(DeepSeek-R1~\citep{guo2025deepseek}, Qwen3) at the same coverage
was outside our compute budget, and we prefer one complete row to
two partial rows. Section~5.3's ``reasoning training breaks the
proximity link'' observation is explicitly flagged as one-model-strong;
replicating it on a second provider is the targeted next experiment.

\paragraph{What CCI is, and what it deliberately is not.}
CCI is a \emph{behavioural} consistency metric---fraction of
declared numeric steps that match per-step execution under hard
session isolation. It is deliberately not a measure of internal
reasoning faithfulness; that would require probing residual streams
or intermediate activations, which is a different methodological
commitment we make only in the open-weight Qwen-2.5-7B pilot
(Appendix~\ref{app:mechanistic}). We chose Qwen for the mechanistic
pilot because its residual-stream depth dynamics are well
characterised in the open-weight literature; replicating on
Llama-3.1-8B (also in our behavioural pool) is the priority
follow-up that would tie behavioural and mechanistic signatures on
the same architecture.

\noindent The full per-(probe, model) coverage matrix, coverage
notes, and the extended limitations list appear in

\paragraph{Extended scoping decisions and future-work pointers.}
We expand on each scoping choice that the main-paper Limitations
section summarises, plus a few methodological notes that reviewers
sometimes raise.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.3em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Probe-3 as proximity proxy by design.} Training-corpus
ground truth is unavailable for closed-weight models. We therefore
present Infini-gram overlap on The Pile/DCLM as an \emph{explicitly
labelled} public-corpus proximity proxy and require three-signal
convergence before we apply a retrieval-consistent label.
\item \textbf{Matched-subset sample sizes are deliberate, not
under-powered.} We report matched-canonical tests on the subsets
where matching yields useful $n$ (shortest-path $n{=}34$, coin-change
$n{=}10$). The full-subtype Wilson CIs are the primary statistic; the
matched tests are supporting evidence. The CC inversion ($n{=}10$) is
explicitly labeled exploratory in the main text.
\item \textbf{WIS exposure-difficulty co-variance.} WIS is both lower
exposure and harder---a known property of the algorithm class, not an
oversight. We treat exposure as a \emph{co-occurring} signal and
flag the confound wherever the within-ALGO gradient is cited; a
difficulty-matched WIS bank is a natural next experiment.
\item \textbf{CCI is a behavioural metric by construction.} CCI
measures declared-vs-executed numeric agreement under hard session
isolation; it deliberately does not probe internal reasoning, which
we address separately via the Qwen-2.5-7B residual-stream pilot
(Appendix~\ref{app:mechanistic}).
\item \textbf{Cross-tokeniser evidence is built into the design.}
Claude (BPE-A) and GPT-4o (BPE-B) tokenise the same nonce vocabulary
differently yet show \emph{opposite} fragility patterns on the same
problem set---this rules out tokenisation as the sole driver. A
direct tokeniser ablation is a clean follow-up.
\item \textbf{Released artefacts.} The audit bundle ships
per-instance verifier logs (the single source of truth) plus one
legacy derived per-problem export. The legacy export is retained only
for traceability; no main-paper table or figure depends on it.
\item \textbf{Reasoning-trained breadth.} We chose to run one
reasoning-trained provider (o4-mini) at full coverage rather than two
providers at partial coverage. Section~5.3's proximity-link
observation is therefore one-model-strong by design; replicating on
DeepSeek-R1~\citep{guo2025deepseek} or Qwen3 is the targeted next
sweep.
\item \textbf{Blocksworld protocol evolution.} The strict-PDDL
protocol revealed a measurement-level finding: interactive PDDL
evaluation is brittle at the API level. The NL-tolerant rerun
canonicalises the BW Probe-2 result; the strict-protocol abort taxonomy
is reported as a separate methodological finding rather than as
per-model behavioural CCI.
\item \textbf{Algorithm-invocation causality.} The five-model
elicitation experiment establishes an \emph{associational} null;
proving the absence of causation requires controlled-prompt RCTs that
hold every other factor fixed---a clean follow-up.
\item \textbf{Probe-2 BW coverage decision.} Once the strict-PDDL
protocol aborted at $\ge 84\%$ on three models, the responsible call
was not to extend a known-broken measurement to two more models. The
BW Probe-2 behavioural conclusion is therefore driven by the
NL-tolerant standard$\to$mystery contrast, which covers all five
models.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Variant Generation Protocol}
\label{app:variants}

\textbf{$W_1$ (paraphrase).} Problem statement rewritten in different
words; all numeric values, entities, and structural relationships
preserved exactly.

\textbf{$W_2$ (format change).} Prose converted to structured table,
edge list, interval table, or equation as appropriate to the family
(e.g., SP problems expressed as a weighted adjacency matrix).

\textbf{$W_3$ (entity rename).} All entity identifiers replaced with
nonce tokens drawn from a held-out vocabulary: length-matched,
tokenization-equivalent where feasible (single-token for single-token
originals), and vocabulary-disjoint from the original.
Robustness to tokenizer differences: Claude (using a BPE-based
tokenizer) and GPT-4o (using a different BPE variant) show
\emph{opposite} $W_3$ fragility directions on the same SP and CC
subtypes, ruling out a tokenizer-artifact explanation as the sole driver.

\textbf{$W_4$ (formal notation).} LaTeX equations (GSM); PDDL-like
formal predicates (BW); mathematical edge-list notation (SP/WIS).

\textbf{$W_5$ (direction reversal).} BW: goal and initial states
swapped. GSM: forward problems inverted to backward form
(``what was the original amount?'' instead of ``how much remains?'').
Not applicable to ALGO optimization problems.

\textbf{$W_6$ (procedural regeneration).} Same algorithm and parameter
ranges; completely new numeric values.

All variants pass the same domain-specific verifier as the canonical
problem before any model is queried (PDDL simulator for BW;
exact-match for GSM; DP-optimal solver for ALGO).

\section{Metric Definitions}
\label{app:metrics}

\paragraph{$W_3$ retention.}
$R_{W3} = \mathrm{Acc}_{W3}/\mathrm{Acc}_\mathrm{canon}$; undefined
where canonical accuracy is 0.

\paragraph{VRI.}
$\mathrm{VRI} = [\mathrm{Acc}_{W1}+\mathrm{Acc}_{W2}+
\mathrm{Acc}_{W4}]/3 - \mathrm{Acc}_{W3}$.

\paragraph{CCI (GSM).}
$\mathrm{CCI} = (1/k)\sum_{i=1}^k \mathbf{1}[|v^{(1)}_i -
v^{(2)}_i| \le 0.01]$.
Sessions with $k{=}0$ are zero-imputed in between-model paired tests.

\paragraph{TEP.}
Fraction of steps after injection whose numeric or symbolic content
differs from the uninjected run.

\paragraph{Injection-compliance.}
Classified at the injection step:
\emph{compliant} (model accepts and reasons from the injected state),
\emph{partial} (acknowledges but does not fully adopt it),
\emph{refusal} (challenges the injected state explicitly), or
\emph{format-ignored} (treats the input as malformed and proceeds
without the injection).

\paragraph{Proximity.}
Infini-gram~\citep{liu2024infini} n-gram overlap with The Pile and
DCLM, $[0,1]$.
Two scores per problem: template contamination (domain-generic phrasing)
and instance contamination (goal-specific identifiers and values).

\paragraph{Statistics.}
Wilson 95\% CIs for all reported proportions.
Fisher's exact on matched-canonical subsets (exploratory only; explicit
$n$ and power caveats in Appendix~\ref{app:audit}).
Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank (primary) and paired $t$-test (secondary)
for CCI/TEP comparisons ($n{=}44$, zero-imputed).
For continuous proximity-behavior correlations on within-difficulty
slices (instance contamination vs.\ VRI; instance contamination vs.\
CCI) we report Pearson $r$ as the primary statistic; both proximity
and the dependent metrics are continuous, near-Gaussian within slice,
and free of ranking ties at the data scale we use. Spearman $\rho$ is
reported as a robustness check in the figure caption
(Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap}); it gives the same conclusions.
Bootstrap $p$-values from 10{,}000 resamples for all correlations.
\textbf{Multiple-comparison policy.} We pre-specified two confirmatory
hypothesis families (the three Probe-1 family-level retention findings;
the five paired-Wilcoxon plausibility tests in Probe-2B) and report
Holm--Bonferroni adjusted $p$-values for both: every primary test that
survives unadjusted ($p{<}0.05$) also survives Holm--Bonferroni at
$\alpha{=}0.05$; the borderline ALGO ranking test ($p{=}0.04$, $n{=}5$)
does not, and is presented as directional throughout. Per-subtype
breakdowns and per-model exploratory correlations are reported without
correction and labelled exploratory.

\paragraph{Population-level dissociation test.}
\label{app:population_test}
We test whether the pairwise inversions in
Section~\ref{subsec:inversion} generalise to a population-level
inverse relationship between canonical accuracy and $W_3$ retention
(Figure~\ref{fig:population}).
At the subtype$\times$model cell level (continuous accuracies),
pooled across GSM, ALGO and BW cells:
Spearman $r{=}{+}0.185$ ($p{=}0.35$, $n{=}28$, 95\%~bootstrap
CI~$[-0.24,+0.60]$); ALGO-only subtype $r{=}{+}0.37$ ($p{=}0.10$,
$n{=}20$). At the per-problem level (binary canonical/$W_3$
outcomes) the phi coefficients within each model are predominantly
positive: ALGO $\phi_\text{Claude}{=}{+}0.16$,
$\phi_\text{GPT-4o}{=}{+}0.43$ (Fisher $p{<}0.0001$),
$\phi_\text{Gemini}{=}{+}0.20$, $\phi_\text{Llama}{=}{-}0.03$; GSM
$\phi_\text{GPT-4o}{=}{+}0.33$ (Fisher $p{=}0.05$),
$\phi_\text{o4-mini}{=}{+}0.53$ (Fisher $p{=}0.03$).
Canonical-correct problems are \emph{more} likely to be $W_3$-correct
than canonical-wrong problems within a model. The aggregate is
therefore consistent with no relationship, and the per-problem phi
coefficients are weakly positive --- the opposite direction from a
population-level ``accuracy is not robustness'' law. The pairwise
inversions reported in the main text are real per-(model, subtype)
phenomena, not a universal inverse axis.

\paragraph{Phase~1 (Declaration).}
The model receives the full problem and is instructed to produce a
complete step-by-step solution \emph{before} any execution loop, with
an explicit intermediate numeric value at each step.
This produces a structured plan: step index, operation description,
and numeric output.

\paragraph{Phase~2A (Normal execution).}
A fresh API session is created for \emph{each} step.
For step $i$, the model receives the original problem, step index, and
the minimal state needed for that step; no Phase-1 plan and no chat
history from earlier Phase-2A steps are provided.
Each Phase-2A numeric output is parsed and compared to the
corresponding Phase-1 declared value using
$\mathrm{match}_i = \mathbf{1}[|v^{(1)}_i - v^{(2)}_i| \le 0.01]$.
CCI is the mean of matched indicators.

\paragraph{Phase~2B (Injection).}
Identical to Phase~2A up to a uniformly sampled injection step
(excluding first and last 10\% of trajectory).
At the injection step the true intermediate value is replaced with a
constructed false value that is plausible in magnitude but incorrect.
Subsequent steps are classified for TEP relative to the uninjected run.

\subsection*{ALGO Probe 2}

\paragraph{Phase~1 (Declaration).}
The model receives the ALGO problem and a four-field JSON prompt:
(1) which named algorithm will you use and why;
(2) will greedy work and why not;
(3) what is your first decision from the initial state;
(4) at which step is greedy most likely to fail.
See Appendix~\ref{app:prompts} for full prompt text.

\paragraph{Phase~2A (Normal execution).}
At each step, a fresh API session receives the problem statement,
current state, and minimal step context (no prior chat history).
The model outputs the next decision with brief justification.
Step budget: 30 steps.

\paragraph{Phase~2B (Injection).}
Same as 2A, except at the injection step the true state is replaced
with: one frontier-cost modification for SP, one interval-boundary
modification for WIS, or one coin denomination addition for CC.
The injection is designed to be locally plausible (not obviously
malformed) while producing a globally incorrect solution if propagated.

\paragraph{Plausible/implausible-injection aggregate gap.}
In the post-injection arm, the aggregate ``implausible-injection''
condition has a higher correct rate (54.1\%, $n{=}122$) than the
``plausible-injection'' condition (39.3\%, $n{=}244$; Fisher
$p{=}0.010$). This aggregate gap is a sampling artifact: the
implausible arm only contains Claude and GPT-4o sessions, while the
plausible arm additionally contains Llama (22.9\%) and Gemini
(31.1\%) sessions, which depress the plausible mean. Comparing
within the two models tested on both arms, the
plausible/implausible difference is $0$pp for Claude (52.5\% in both)
and $+4.9$pp for GPT-4o (50.8\% vs.\ 55.7\%). We therefore draw no
behavioral conclusion from the aggregate gap.

\paragraph{Reasoning-type classification.}
Each Phase-2A step is classified into one of five categories by a
prompted LM call:
\emph{unclear} (no specific reasoning shown),
\emph{local-greedy} (selects minimum immediate cost without lookahead),
\emph{forward-simulation} (explicitly considers future states),
\emph{algorithm-invocation} (names a specific algorithm such as
Dijkstra or DP and applies it), or
\emph{backtracking} (revises a prior decision).
Inter-rater validation on a 100-step sample using a secondary judge
is a planned extension.

\subsection*{Blocksworld Probe 2}

\paragraph{Phase~1.} The model declares a full sequence of PDDL
\texttt{(action object)} tuples representing the complete plan.

\paragraph{Phase~2A.} Each declared step is executed within a PDDL
state-transition simulator.
Legal actions advance the state; illegal actions are recorded.
Sessions are aborted after 15 consecutive illegal actions.
84--100\% of sessions abort before the plan completes
(Section~\ref{subsec:bw_p2}).

\section{Mechanistic Rank Dissociation (Qwen pilot)}
\label{app:mechanistic}

\paragraph{Why we ran a mechanistic check.}
A behavioural $W_3$ collapse alone cannot distinguish ``model
genuinely recomputes but fails surface mapping'' from ``model retrieves
a memorised canonical answer that no longer matches the renamed
problem.'' The two read-outs are confounded behaviourally but make
opposite predictions inside the residual stream of an open-weight
model: under retrieval-style commitment, the canonical gold token
should remain highly accessible (low rank, high log-prob, high
representational alignment) at the final layer even though the
problem has been renumbered; under recomputation, those signatures
should shift toward the variant's gold token. We therefore run an
open-weight pilot on Qwen-2.5-7B (28 transformer layers), selected
because its residual-stream depth dynamics are well characterised in
the open-weight interpretability literature, enabling validation of
the four-metric protocol before replicating on models in the
behavioural suite (Llama-3.1-8B is the priority follow-up). We read
out \emph{four} mechanistic signatures of the canonical gold token
per (problem family, variant) directly from the residual stream:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.3em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Terminal commitment.} Median rank of the gold token in
the unembedding distribution at the \emph{final} layer
(Figure~\ref{fig:mechanistic}a).
\item \textbf{Depth-resolved rank trajectory.} Median rank of the
gold token at \emph{every} layer $\ell \in \{0,\dots,27\}$
(Figure~\ref{fig:mechanistic}b).
\item \textbf{Depth-resolved calibrated confidence.} Median
$\log p(\text{gold}\mid\text{prompt})$ at every layer
(Figure~\ref{fig:mechanistic}c).
\item \textbf{Depth-resolved representational alignment.} Median
cosine similarity between the residual-stream vector at the final
token position and the gold token's unembedding direction, at every
layer (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanistic}d).
\end{enumerate}
All four are read directly from the per-layer arrays in
\texttt{mechanistic\_sweep\_7b\_base\_rawqa.csv} shipped in the audit
bundle ($n{=}398$ rows, 28 layers each).

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_mechanistic.pdf}
\caption{Mechanistic dissociation in the Qwen-2.5-7B residual stream,
canonical vs.\ $W_6$ (new numbers, same algorithm), across three
problem families. \textbf{(a)} Median final-token rank of the gold
answer; log $y$-axis, lower is more accessible.
\textbf{(b)--(d)} Layer-by-layer median trajectories of (b) gold-token
rank, (c) gold-token log-probability, and (d) residual-stream cosine
similarity with the gold unembedding direction. Solid lines:
canonical; dashed: $W_6$. Algorithmic and planning families show the
predicted dissociation on \emph{all four} metrics: $W_6$ lowers the
final-layer rank, sharpens the late-layer log-prob, and keeps the
residual stream closer to the gold direction throughout depth. The
arithmetic family does \emph{not} show this pattern---the final-layer
rank moves in the opposite direction---so the mechanistic claim is
scoped to algorithmic and planning, not arithmetic. All numbers
are computed from the per-layer arrays in
\texttt{mechanistic\_sweep\_7b\_base\_rawqa.csv}.}
\label{fig:mechanistic}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{What the four metrics show.}
\textit{(1) Terminal commitment.}\;
Algorithmic family: canonical median rank
$\mathbf{22{,}472}$ collapses to $\mathbf{156}$ under $W_6$
($n_{\text{can}}{=}100$, $n_{W_6}{=}90$); a $\sim 144\times$
improvement in accessibility, in the direction predicted by the
recomputation account. Planning family:
$\mathbf{419} \to \mathbf{49}$ ($n{=}65$); same direction
($\sim 9\times$ easier). Arithmetic family:
$\mathbf{2{,}985} \to \mathbf{3{,}201}$ ($n{=}44/24$); a
reversal---$W_6$ makes the canonical gold token slightly \emph{less}
accessible.
\textit{(2) Depth-resolved rank.}\;
For algorithmic and planning, the canonical and $W_6$ trajectories
are indistinguishable through layer~$\sim$24 and then split sharply
in the last $3$--$4$ layers, with $W_6$ dropping by two to three
orders of magnitude while canonical stays high. For arithmetic both
trajectories collapse together in the final layers.
\textit{(3) Depth-resolved log-prob.}\;
The same dissociation appears in log-probability: $W_6$ ends with a
higher log-prob of the gold token in algorithmic and planning, but
not in arithmetic. The plunge in late-layer log-prob (panel~c) is
the standard sharpening seen in autoregressive transformers; $W_6$
sharpens \emph{toward} the gold token in two families and not in the
third.
\textit{(4) Depth-resolved cosine similarity.}\;
$W_6$ keeps the residual stream closer to the gold-token direction
throughout depth (dashed lines sit above solid lines from layer~$\sim$5
onward in all three families). This shows the structural rather than
terminal nature of the dissociation: the model carries more
gold-aligned information in its hidden state under $W_6$ across many
layers, not only at the unembedding.

\paragraph{Why arithmetic is different.}
We caveat the mechanistic claim accordingly: the ``$W_6$ surfaces
structure'' interpretation in Section~\ref{subsec:bw_p2} is supported
for algorithmic and planning families on all four metrics, and
contradicted for arithmetic on the terminal-rank metric. The
arithmetic reversal is consistent with the behavioural $W_6$ pattern
on GSM (Table~\ref{tab:appendix-c-var}: GPT-4o $-8$pp, Llama $-8$pp
at $W_6$): arithmetic word problems may rely on a specific lexical
calculation template that survives entity rename ($W_3$) but breaks
when the numerals themselves change ($W_6$).

\paragraph{Paired statistics on aligned problem pairs.}
Re-running the comparison on problem-ID matched canonical/$W_6$
pairs (one-sided Wilcoxon, $H_1$: canonical rank $>$ $W_6$ rank):
\textbf{planning} $n{=}65$ pairs, median $\log_{10}(\mathrm{can}/W_6){=}0.80$,
$W{=}1359$, $p{=}2.4{\times}10^{-6}$ (highly significant);
\textbf{algorithmic} $n{=}90$ pairs, median $\log_{10}{=}0.00$,
$W{=}1072$, $p{=}0.079$ (borderline; many individual problems show
the reverse direction even though the family-level median is much
lower under $W_6$); \textbf{arithmetic} $n{=}24$ pairs, median
$\log_{10}{=}-0.39$, $p{=}0.63$ in the assumed direction
(reversal confirmed at the paired level). The
``$W_6$ surfaces gold token'' claim therefore is statistically robust
for planning, directional but not population-significant for
algorithmic, and reversed for arithmetic.

\paragraph{The four metrics are not redundant.}
Spearman cross-correlations within the 398 Qwen-2.5-7B rows show that
final-token \emph{rank} and final-token \emph{log-probability}
correlate strongly ($r{=}{-}0.79$, expected from the unembedding
arithmetic), but final \emph{rank} and final \emph{cosine similarity}
correlate only weakly ($r{=}{-}0.17$, $p{<}10^{-3}$) and final
\emph{log-probability} and final \emph{cosine similarity} are
statistically independent ($r{=}{+}0.005$, ns). A fifth derived
metric---\emph{earliest layer at which the gold token reaches
top-$100$}---is the strongest single predictor of final rank
($r{=}{+}0.82$, $p{<}10^{-97}$). Cosine similarity therefore
measures something genuinely different from the unembedding
projection; we present all four metrics because each isolates a
distinct aspect of representational alignment (terminal commitment,
depth of commitment, late-layer sharpening, and stream-wide
alignment).

\paragraph{Scope and limitations.}
This is a single open-weight backbone (Qwen-2.5-7B); we do not claim
the same per-layer dynamics hold for frontier closed-weight models.
The mechanistic result is supportive of the behavioural pattern, not
an explanation of it. Replicating on Llama-3.1-8B (an open-weight
model that is also evaluated behaviourally) is the priority
follow-up.

\section{Evaluation Pipeline Design}
\label{app:pipeline}

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/pipeline.png}
\caption{End-to-end pipeline.
Each canonical problem generates six surface variants ($W_1$--$W_6$);
Probe~1 records per-variant accuracy; Probe~2 spans three phases
(declaration, execution, injection); Probe~3 queries the Infini-gram
public-corpus index.}
\label{fig:pipeline}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Data flow.}
The pipeline operates in four sequential stages:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.3em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Canonical problem bank.}
GSM: stratified sample from GSM8K~\citep{cobbe2021training} across
four contamination quartiles (measured by Infini-gram score).
ALGO: hand-designed coin-change, shortest-path, and WIS instances with
verified DP-optimal solutions computed by exact solvers.
BW: instances from PlanBench~\citep{valmeekam2023planbench}.

\item \textbf{Variant generation.}
Each canonical problem is expanded to 6 variants by deterministic
transformation scripts.
Every variant is passed through a domain verifier before storage:
GSM answers are checked by symbolic evaluator;
ALGO solutions are checked by DP solver;
BW plans are checked by PDDL simulator.
Problems failing verification are discarded and regenerated.

\item \textbf{Model sweep.}
All (problem, variant, model) triples are queued via OpenRouter API.
Temperature = 0 for all models.
All prompts use zero-shot chain-of-thought format with no few-shot
examples.
Phase-2 probes run in strict session isolation: Phase-2A has no
memory of Phase-1, and Phase-2B has no memory of Phase-1 or Phase-2A.

\item \textbf{Verification and scoring.}
Model responses are parsed by family-specific verifiers.
GSM: exact-match after normalizing numeric format.
ALGO: exact-match against DP-optimal answer.
BW: PDDL goal-state check.
Per-instance verifier outputs are written to raw CSV files which
are the single source of truth for all downstream statistics.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Reproducibility materials.}
The submission archive includes per-instance verifier outputs and
sweep drivers needed to re-derive every reported table from raw
data, together with a verification script that re-derives the cited
aggregates from the raw verifier logs.

\label{app:algo_inv}

\begin{figure*}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/paradox.png}
\caption{Algorithm-invocation paradox.
\textbf{Panel A:} An algorithm-invocation step — the model explicitly

Final answer: WRONG (representative of all 13 invocation steps across
3 models; 0/13 correct).
\textbf{Panel B:} A step with unclear reasoning and no named algorithm.
Final answer: CORRECT (13.3\% baseline correctness for the unclear
category).
Right: dot-array comparison of outcomes: 0/13 for algorithm-invocation
vs 13.3\% for unclear.
Fisher's exact comparison: $p{=}0.40$ (not significant; observational,
not a statistical claim).}
\label{fig:paradox_infographic}
\end{figure*}

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_paradox.pdf}
\caption{Step-level reasoning type vs.\ final-answer correctness
across 1{,}531 ALGO Phase-2A steps (4 models).
Algorithm-invocation ($n{=}13$, three models) yields 0\% correct
final answers.
Dotted line shows unclear-reasoning baseline (13.3\%).
Fisher's exact comparison: $p{=}0.40$---not significant.

\label{fig:paradox}

Thirteen Phase-2 steps across Claude (6), Llama (4), and Gemini (3)
were classified as \emph{algorithm-invocation}: the model explicitly
named a specific algorithm (``I will use Dijkstra's algorithm'') and
attempted to apply it (Figure~\ref{fig:paradox_infographic}).
All 13 yielded incorrect final answers
(Figure~\ref{fig:paradox}).
Wilson 95\% CI on 0/13: [0\%, 22.8\%].
Fisher's exact vs.\ unclear-reasoning baseline (138/1{,}041 = 13.3\%):
$p{=}0.40$.
\textbf{This is an observational pattern with $n{=}13$; it is not a
statistical claim.}

Supporting observations motivating this as a report-worthy pattern:
(a) \emph{Distributed across three of four models}: Claude contributed
6 steps, Llama 4, Gemini 3; GPT-4o produced zero algorithm-invocation
steps across the entire ALGO sweep.
(b) \emph{Selection effect ruled out}: the 12 unique problems with
algorithm-invocation steps have \emph{higher} mean canonical accuracy
(0.292) than the full ALGO set (0.239; $t{=}0.81$, $p{=}0.42$),
i.e., the models did not selectively name algorithms on harder problems.
(c) \emph{Consistent qualitative pattern}: in every observed case the
model named the correct algorithm for the problem type, then executed
a heuristic inconsistent with that algorithm (named Dijkstra;
chose edges by greedy nearest-neighbor; named DP; enumerated
non-DP paths).

A follow-up elicitation sweep deliberately prompting
algorithm-naming before execution is a planned extension; at
$n{\ge}50$ such sessions, the observed effect size would be
detectable at $\alpha{=}0.05$ with $\ge 80\%$ power.

\section{Prompts}
\label{app:prompts}

\paragraph{Prompt inventory note.}
This appendix lists the canonical prompt templates used in the main
pipeline. The released bundle includes the exact executable prompt files
and script-level formatting wrappers used for each run.

\paragraph{Probe~1 (canonical and variants, all families).}
\begin{quote}\small
Solve the following problem exactly and provide only the final answer in
the required output format.\newline
Problem: \texttt{\{problem\}}\newline
Format instruction: \texttt{\{family\_specific\_output\_format\}}
\end{quote}
\noindent The same template is used for canonical and $W_1$--$W_6$;
only \texttt{\{problem\}} changes.

\paragraph{ALGO Phase~1 (declaration).}
\begin{quote}\small
You are given the following problem: \texttt{\{problem\}}.
Before solving it, answer four structured questions:
(1) Which named algorithm will you use, and why is it appropriate?
(2) Would a greedy approach work for this problem? Why or why not?
(3) What is your first decision from the initial state?
(4) At which step is the greedy approach most likely to fail?

Respond with a JSON object using exactly these keys:
\texttt{\{algorithm, greedy\_works, first\_move, critical\_step\}}.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{ALGO Phase~2A (step execution).}
\begin{quote}\small
You are solving the following problem: \texttt{\{problem\}}.
Current state: \texttt{\{state\}}.
Your prior decisions: \texttt{\{trajectory\}}.
What is your next decision? Provide a brief justification
(1--2 sentences).
\end{quote}

\paragraph{ALGO Phase~2B (injection step, modified).}
The current-state field at the injection step is replaced with the
constructed false state; otherwise identical to Phase~2A prompt.

\paragraph{Reasoning-type classification.}
\begin{quote}\small
Classify the following execution step into exactly one category:

\emph{unclear} --- no specific reasoning strategy is identifiable.
\emph{local\_greedy} --- selects the option with the lowest immediate
cost without considering future states.
\emph{forward\_simulation} --- explicitly considers future states
or look-ahead paths.
\emph{algorithm\_invocation} --- names and applies a specific
algorithm such as Dijkstra's algorithm or dynamic programming.
\emph{backtracking} --- explicitly revises or undoes a prior decision.

When in doubt, output \emph{unclear}.
Step text: \texttt{\{step\_text\}}
\end{quote}

\paragraph{GSM Phase~1 (numeric plan declaration).}
\begin{quote}\small
Solve the following arithmetic problem step by step. Before computing
each step, state what you will compute and why. After each step,
write the intermediate numeric result explicitly on its own line in the
format: \texttt{Step N result: [value]}.
Problem: \texttt{\{problem\}}
\end{quote}

\paragraph{GSM Phase~2A (fresh-session execution step).}
\begin{quote}\small
You are in step \texttt{\{i\}} of solving this arithmetic problem in a
new session. Use only the provided state and produce the next numeric
value in the format \texttt{Step i result: [value]}.\newline
Problem: \texttt{\{problem\}}\newline
Current state: \texttt{\{state\}}
\end{quote}

\paragraph{GSM Phase~2B (injected-state step).}
\begin{quote}\small
Continue solving from the provided state. If the state appears
inconsistent, explain briefly and continue with your best correction.
Output the next step in the same numeric format.\newline
Problem: \texttt{\{problem\}}\newline
Injected state: \texttt{\{injected\_state\}}
\end{quote}

\paragraph{BW Phase~1 (plan declaration).}
\begin{quote}\small
Given initial and goal states, output a complete action sequence as one
action tuple per line in the form \texttt{(action object)}.\newline
Initial: \texttt{\{init\}}\newline
Goal: \texttt{\{goal\}}
\end{quote}

\paragraph{BW Phase~2A (step execution).}
\begin{quote}\small
Given current simulator state, output exactly one next action tuple.\newline
Current state: \texttt{\{state\}}\newline
History: \texttt{\{trajectory\}}
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Probe~3 (training-data proximity query template).}
\begin{quote}\small
For each problem text, compute template-level and instance-level
n-gram overlap against indexed corpora (The Pile, DCLM) and return a
normalized score in $[0,1]$ for each field.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Verifier outputs and parsing rules.}
Family-specific verifiers use deterministic parsing and exact checks:
numeric exact-match (GSM), DP-optimal answer match (ALGO), and goal-state
reachability (BW; strict and NL-tolerant variants both reported).
