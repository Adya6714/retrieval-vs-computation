\begin{abstract}
When two language models achieve the same score on a benchmark, are they
solving problems the same way? We show they are not: identical accuracy can
be reached through genuine computation or surface pattern recognition, and
the two strategies only become distinguishable \emph{beyond} the accuracy
number. We apply three independent diagnostic probes---surface rename,
cross-session plan-vs-execution consistency, and training-data
proximity---to 219 reasoning problems across arithmetic, planning, and
algorithmic optimization, running five models (including the
reasoning-trained \omini) for approximately 20{,}000 API calls. Three-probe
convergence labels apply most completely to the arithmetic and
algorithmic-optimization families; on Blocksworld our strict PDDL protocol
aborts in 84--100\% of sessions (itself a measurement-protocol finding), so
the Blocksworld execution probe is reported via a parsing-tolerant rerun with
limited convergence coverage. Three findings
stand out. First, the same block-rename operation \emph{improves} two
models, \emph{destroys} one, and \emph{leaves two untouched}---on identical
problems. Second, weighted interval scheduling collapses every model to zero
under entity rename despite near-perfect canonical accuracy, isolating
algorithmic depth as a robustness axis orthogonal to benchmark score. Third,
models accept injected wrong intermediate states at high rates yet still
recover the correct final answer roughly half the time, showing that
compliance and derailment are empirically separable. None of these patterns
is visible from benchmark accuracy alone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Imagine two students score 80\% on the same exam. You rename every person in
every word problem; numbers and structure are unchanged. Student A still
scores 80\%; Student B drops to 0\%. Who actually understood the material?
This is, roughly, what we observe in frontier LLMs: two models can reach
identical benchmark accuracy via fundamentally different strategies, and
standard accuracy reporting cannot tell them apart.

\paragraph{The surprising finding.}
On shortest-path planning, Claude Sonnet~4 scores 64.7\% vs.\ GPT-4o's
41.2\%, then drops to 0.0\% under pure entity renaming while GPT-4o retains
26.5\% (matched SP subset, $n{=}34$, Fisher $p{=}0.0021$). This is not a
two-model anecdote: across Claude, GPT-4o, Gemini, and
Llama~\citep{dubey2024llama}, robustness ordering is family-dependent and
cannot be inferred from canonical accuracy. (A same-direction coin-change
reversal at $n{=}10$ is reported as exploratory in Appendix~\ref{app:cov}.)

\paragraph{Why one test is not enough.}
A drop under renaming alone admits mundane explanations (tokenization,
residual difficulty, noise). Our approach is \emph{convergence}: three
independent tests with non-overlapping assumptions. Prior work
\citep{mirzadeh2024gsm,wu2024reasoning,wang2024rupbench} reports group-level
drops; we diagnose individual instances by asking whether all three signals
agree.

\paragraph{What is genuinely new.}
Two design choices (Table~\ref{tab:novelty}): cross-session
plan-vs-execution under hard isolation (CCI; neither within-context
\citep{turpin2023language} nor own-CoT perturbation
\citep{lanham2023measuring}); and a built-in DP exposure control (CC/SP/WIS
share algorithm, differ in public-corpus prevalence).

\paragraph{The three probes.}
Three orthogonal diagnostics---surface rename (\wthree), cross-session
plan-vs-execution (CCI/TEP under hard session isolation), and Infini-gram
public-corpus proximity~\citep{liu2024infini}---apply to every problem
(Figure~\ref{fig:framework}; metrics in Section~\ref{sec:metrics}; full
pipeline in Appendix~\ref{app:pipeline}).\footnote{Anonymous code and logs:
\url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/retrieval-vs-computation-EA8F/README.md}.}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Surface sensitivity.}
\citet{mirzadeh2024gsm} (GSM-Symbolic) show arithmetic accuracy drops under
template-variable perturbation in a single domain at the group-mean level.
\citet{wu2024reasoning} extend this across 11 counterfactual tasks (again
group-level). \citet{wang2024rupbench} (RUPBench) apply nine perturbation
types over 15 reasoning datasets. \citet{li2024gsm} (GSM-Plus) generate
diverse variants of GSM8K and find consistent accuracy drops;
\citet{huang2025mathperturb} (MATH-Perturb) construct hard perturbations on
competition math. We differ by coupling surface, execution, and exposure
probes \emph{per instance} on shared problems (Table~\ref{tab:novelty}),
with a built-in DP exposure gradient (CC/SP/WIS) and matched-canonical pairs
that invert across models.

\paragraph{Most related concurrent work.}
\citet{kumar2025matcha} (MATCHA) test the decoupling hypothesis on GSM8K
with \emph{within-trace} answer-conditioned perturbations and an LLM
judge---complementary to our deterministic \wthree{} nonce-token rename and
cross-session CCI. The Robust Reasoning Benchmark \citep{rrb2026} applies 13
structural perturbations to competition-math items at group scale but
includes no execution or contamination probes. PutnamGAP
\citep{hao2025putnamgap} stress-tests surface renames and kernel rewrites on
thousands of Putnam problems without per-instance execution or proximity
controls. We position against all three in Table~\ref{tab:novelty}: our
contribution is per-instance triangulation on a fixed 219-problem bank with
session-isolated Probe~2 and Infini-gram proximity on the same instances.

\paragraph{CoT faithfulness.}
Prior work
\citep{turpin2023language,lanham2023measuring,saparov2023language,lightman2023let,tutek2025measuring}
intervenes within a single trace or on model weights. Our CCI compares a
session-A declaration to session-B execution with no shared context
($\eps{=}0.01$ numeric match)---behavioural plan-execution consistency under
hard isolation, not within-trace or parametric faithfulness.

\paragraph{Training-data contamination.}
\citet{razeghi2022impact} show pretraining term frequency predicts
arithmetic accuracy. \citet{golchin2023time} detect contamination via guided
instruction completion. \citet{shi2024detecting} extend contamination
analysis to reasoning benchmarks. For closed-weight models we treat
Infini-gram overlap~\citep{liu2024infini} as a public-corpus proximity
proxy; we discuss its limitations and partial validation via the
open-weight Llama model in Section~\ref{sec:proximity}.

\paragraph{LLM planning and execution.}
\citet{valmeekam2023planning} demonstrate systematic Blocksworld planning
failure. We extend PlanBench~\citep{valmeekam2023planbench} with a
cross-session execution probe and a false-state injection probe, testing
whether models that accept an injected error maintain correct final-answer
generation.

\paragraph{Concurrent work on reasoning-model robustness.}
\citet{vonrecum2026reasoning} perturb models' own CoT tokens within-trace;
our Probe-2B injects external wrong state into fresh sessions with no prior
reasoning context---complementary tests of compliance vs.\ final
correctness.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We test five models---four frontier chat models and the reasoning-trained
\omini---on three types of reasoning problems, each presented in up to seven
surface forms. Every problem has a verified correct answer, and every surface
variant preserves that answer exactly; only the presentation changes. The
core question: does the model still get it right?

\paragraph{Problem families.}
Three reasoning domains, chosen to dissociate three confounds that would
otherwise undermine the probes individually: (i)~\textbf{GSM} (arithmetic)
calibrates the probes against a benchmark with documented contamination
structure but a very different algorithmic style; (ii)~\textbf{BW} (planning)
exposes the format-vs-policy confound (``did the model actually fail, or did
the parser?'') and provides the matched standard/mystery contrast that
directly probes lexical content while holding planning structure fixed;
(iii)~\textbf{ALGO} (algorithmic optimisation) is the only family where we
can hold the underlying algorithm fixed (dynamic programming, DP) while
varying training-text exposure across three orders of magnitude---this is
what makes the same-algorithm vs.\ different-exposure control in
Section~\ref{sec:proximity} possible at all.

GSM (44 GSM8K problems~\citep{cobbe2021training}): arithmetic calibration
with documented contamination structure. BW (65
PlanBench~\citep{valmeekam2023planbench} instances): planning with
standard/mystery lexical contrasts. ALGO (110 DP problems): three subtypes
with shared algorithm but divergent public-corpus exposure---
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Coin change (CC):} minimum number of coins summing to a
  target. Very common in programming tutorials online.
  \item \textbf{Shortest path (SP):} minimum-cost route through a weighted
  graph. Moderately common.
  \item \textbf{Weighted interval scheduling (WIS):} maximum-value set of
  non-overlapping intervals. Rarely phrased this way in public text.
\end{itemize}
Per-instance audit logs are released (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). GSM
calibrates all probes; ALGO holds DP fixed while varying exposure; BW
stress-tests interactive measurement (Section~\ref{sec:bw}). Coverage:
Table~\ref{tab:coverage}. Throughout, BW Probe-1 \emph{accuracy} is scoped to
the $n{=}65$ bank problems; within-model per-problem correlations
(Figure~\ref{fig:within}) instead pool canonical and variant instances per
model, which is why their BW $n$ is larger (up to 109).

\paragraph{Variants and models.}
Each canonical problem has six verified variants (W1--W6;
Appendix~\ref{app:variants}); W3 renames all entities to nonce tokens. Five
models at $T{=}0$ zero-shot CoT via OpenRouter: Claude Sonnet~4 (hereafter
Claude), GPT-4o, Gemini~2.5 Flash (Gemini), Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (Llama,
\citealp{dubey2024llama}), and \omini. We use these short names throughout.
\omini{} was queried in its default API mode (no explicit
reasoning-token budget set; chain-of-thought is internal and not exposed in
the response). \omini{} reaches 1.00 ALGO / 0.841 GSM canonical yet is most
rename-fragile on BW (\wthree{}${=}0.185$)---family-specific weakness
invisible from accuracy alone.

\subsection{Metrics and convergence labels}
\label{sec:metrics}

Each probe excludes confounds the others admit; labels require convergence.
$\Rwiii{=}\mathrm{Acc}_{W_3}/\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{can}}$; \vri{} compares
W3 to vocabulary-preserving variants. \cci{} (Cross-session Consistency
Index) is the fraction of declared numeric steps matching execution
($\eps{=}0.01$); \tep{} (Trajectory Error Propagation) and injection
compliance measure Probe-2B derailment. Infini-gram overlap on The
Pile/DCLM is a public-corpus proximity proxy~\citep{liu2024infini}.
Instances are \emph{retrieval-consistent} only when all three signals align
(and \emph{computation-consistent} when all reverse); under strict rules
$\sim$62\% of ALGO instances stay ambiguous (Section~\ref{sec:triangulation}).
The 12 high-confidence labels are calibration points for the probe
design---showing the signals can co-align---not primary results;
Sections~\ref{sec:inversion}--\ref{sec:bw} report the behavioural findings.

\paragraph{CCI interpretability.}
Phase~1 (full-plan declaration) and Phase~2A (per-step execution) use
different prompt formats by design; CCI therefore confounds inconsistent
reasoning with differential format propensity and parser coverage. Among
GPT-4o's zero-CCI sessions, 17/29 lack extractable declared numeric steps and
therefore receive CCI${=}0$ \emph{by construction}, not by reasoning failure.
Duplicate Phase-2A calls at $T{=}0$ on an audit subset yield identical
outputs for Claude, ruling out API non-determinism as the driver of CCI
spread. We report CCI as a descriptive fingerprint; a prompt-matched ablation
is deferred (Limitations).

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Subtype-specific robustness inversion: the higher-accuracy model is not always the more robust one}
\label{sec:inversion}

Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation} and Table~\ref{tab:gsm_p1}: on GSM,
Claude/Gemini/\omini{} ($n{=}44$) span canonical accuracy 0.841--0.909
with divergent \wthree{} retention ($\Rwiii{=}$
.892\,/\,.575\,/\,1.00). GPT-4o and Llama replicate the pattern directionally
at $n{=}20$ only (Wilson CIs span $\sim$30pp; Appendix~\ref{app:cov})---we
treat them as partial replication, not powered five-model evidence. The
adequately powered headline is ALGO SP-adv below.

\paragraph{The pattern is sharper on algorithmic problems.}
Shortest-path: Claude 64.7\% canonical ([0.48,0.79]) collapses to 0.0\% under
renaming ([0.00,0.10]); GPT-4o 41.2\% ([0.26,0.58]) retains 26.5\%
([0.15,0.43]). The \emph{better} model on the canonical is the \emph{worse}
one under rename. Coin-change reverses the direction: Claude 60\% retention
([0.31,0.83]), GPT-4o 0\% ([0.00,0.28]). Robustness ordering depends on the
problem subtype.

\paragraph{Statistical confidence.}
SP matched-canonical ($n{=}34$): Claude 0/34 vs.\ GPT-4o 9/34 (Fisher
$p{=}0.0021$); CC ($n{=}10$): Claude 6/10 vs.\ GPT-4o 0/10 ($p{=}0.0108$;
exploratory). Within-model phi: ALGO $\phi_{\text{GPT-4o}}{=}{+}0.43$ (Fisher
$p{<}0.001$); GSM $\phi_{\text{\omini}}{=}{+}0.66$ ($p{<}0.001$;
Appendix~\ref{app:cross}, Figure~\ref{fig:within}). Population-level Spearman
is n.s.\ (Appendix~\ref{app:cross}); ALGO model-rank retention trends
positive ($\rho{=}{+}0.90$, $p{=}0.04$, $n{=}5$; Holm n.s.)---the
dissociation is per-(model, subtype), not a universal axis.

\paragraph{Variant asymmetry and WIS.}
W6 (new numbers) stays near canonical while \wthree{} (rename) drops for
higher-retention models (Figure~\ref{fig:pervariant}); 26/110 ALGO problems
are universally \wthree-fragile (12 SP, 11 WIS, 3 CC). The $3{\times}7{\times}5$
grid (Figure~\ref{fig:algo_grid}): every model reaching $\geq 95\%$ WIS
canonical collapses to 0\% under W3 (including \omini{} $1.00{\to}0.00$); SP~W5
(source/destination reversal) floors the suite
($\leq 4\%$). Algorithmic depth is orthogonal to accuracy.

\subsection{Models that declare a plan often do something completely different---and still get the right answer}
\label{sec:cci}

\cci{} is a behavioral fingerprint, not a performance predictor. Claude
matches $\sim$23\% of declared numeric steps vs.\ GPT-4o's $\sim$11\%
(median${=}0$; Wilcoxon $W{=}396$, $p{=}0.0068$, $n{=}44$ zero-imputed
pairs), yet GPT-4o still answers correctly 70.5\% of the time.
Declaration-following does not predict Claude's correctness ($\rho{=}0.14$,
$p{=}0.38$). Cross-model zero-CCI rates: Gemini 10/44 (23\%), Claude 14/44
(32\%), Llama 24/44 (55\%), GPT-4o 29/44 (66\%); among GPT-4o's 29 zero-CCI
sessions, 17/29 declared no extractable steps and 12/29 declared then
diverged---final accuracy is nearly identical in both (0.69 vs.\ 0.73, n.s.),
confirming step declaration and answer production are largely independent.
Claude and GPT-4o are different \emph{kinds} of reasoners; \cci{} is a
fingerprint, not a score. \tep{} (Trajectory Error Propagation,
Table~\ref{tab:gsm_p2}) is high across all models (mean 0.54--0.77): the
injected wrong value propagates and alters many subsequent steps, yet the
final answer still survives at near-baseline rates---which is exactly the
compliance~$\neq$~correctness finding reported below.

\paragraph{Injection compliance $\neq$ final correctness.}
On 61 ALGO injection instances per model, acceptance is high (Claude 88.5\%,
GPT-4o 93.4\%, Llama 39.3\%, \omini{} 100\%; Gemini 0\% compliant) yet
post-injection accuracy stays near uninterrupted rates (Claude 52.5\%
vs.\ 54.1\%; GPT-4o 50.8\% vs.\ 55.7\%; \omini{} 37.7\%
vs.\ 40.9\%)---compliance and correctness are empirically separable
(Figure~\ref{fig:probe2}c). The figure shows the full three-way split
(compliant / partial-refusal / format-ignored): Gemini format-ignores 100\%
of injections (treats the injected state as malformed input and proceeds
without it), accounting for its 0\% compliant rate; Llama splits across
compliant and partial-refusal.

\paragraph{Two five-model nulls.}
Plausible vs.\ implausible injection ($n{=}61$ paired): no model differs
significantly (Claude $\Delta{=}0.0$pp, $p{=}1.00$; GPT-4o ${+}4.9$pp,
$p{=}0.50$; Llama ${+}3.3$pp, $p{=}0.69$; Gemini ${-}3.3$pp, $p{=}0.64$;
\omini{} ${+}4.9$pp, $p{=}0.48$; Figure~\ref{fig:plausible}). Algorithm
elicitation raises invocation $10$--$50\times$ but not accuracy in any model
(mean $\Delta{=}{-}2.0$pp; Fisher $p{=}0.21$--$0.83$): Claude
$0.500{\to}0.459$, GPT-4o $0.500{\to}0.518$, Gemini $0.300{\to}0.311$, Llama
$0.218{\to}0.148$, \omini{} $0.436{\to}0.418$.

\subsection{Problems the model has likely seen before are easier---but fragile under name changes}
\label{sec:proximity}

At zero template proximity (WIS), \wthree{} robustness is zero for every
model---consistent with both lower exposure and higher baseline difficulty;
we cannot disentangle these from the current design (Table~\ref{tab:proximity}).
CC retains partial robustness for Claude despite shared DP structure.
Proximity--\vri{} correlations use the 64-problem adversarial pool only (the
union of challenging-subtype instances across CC, SP, and WIS).
Proximity predicts per-problem \vri{} for Claude ($r{=}{+}0.44$,
$p{=}0.0003$) and GPT-4o ($r{=}{+}0.37$, $p{=}0.003$) but not Llama/Gemini
($r{\approx}0.12$, n.s.). After residualizing on per-problem canonical
accuracy, partial correlations survive for Claude ($r{=}{+}0.41$,
$p{=}0.0007$) and GPT-4o ($r{=}{+}0.39$, $p{=}0.002$; $n{=}64$), supporting a
proximity-linked rename-fragility association within difficulty tier rather
than a pure floor effect. Proximity also predicts Claude's CCI rigidity
($r{=}{+}0.31$, $p{=}0.044$, $n{=}42$; 2 sessions dropped for missing
proximity scores) independent of correctness
($r{=}{-}0.11$, n.s.). \omini{} breaks the link ($r{=}{-}0.094$, $p{=}0.46$,
$n{=}64$; one-model observation). Figure~\ref{fig:macro} summarises macro
metrics and correlations.

\subsection{Blocksworld: when the measurement protocol itself breaks down}
\label{sec:bw}

Under interactive PDDL, 84--100\% of BW Phase-2A sessions abort (Claude 16\%
/ GPT-4o 0\% / Llama 2\% complete)---a protocol limit, not a model CCI result
(Figure~\ref{fig:bw_profile}: Claude semantic validity 0.68; GPT-4o format
failures 25.6/session; Llama validity 0.27). The NL-tolerant rerun lifts
standard-BW from 0/150 to 14/150 sessions (Fisher
$p{=}8.9{\times}10^{-5}$);\footnote{Standard-BW comprises 50 problems
$\times$ 3 models run under the strict protocol $=150$ sessions; Mystery-BW
comprises 15 problems $\times$ 3 models $=45$ sessions. Counts in this
section are over \emph{sessions}, not unique problems.} Mystery-BW stays
0/45, aligning with the Qwen-2.5-7B mechanistic
pilot~\citep{qwen2025technical} (Appendix~\ref{app:mech}). Paired Wilcoxon on W5-rename under the NL-tolerant protocol
(Figure~\ref{fig:bw_rename}; note these differ from the strict-protocol
values in Table~\ref{tab:pervariant}): Claude $0.422{\to}0.661$
(${+}23.9$pp, $p{=}1.0{\times}10^{-4}$); Gemini $0.385{\to}0.569$
(${+}18.5$pp, $p{=}0.014$); Llama $0.321{\to}0.101$ (${-}22.0$pp,
$p{<}10^{-4}$); GPT-4o/\omini{} unchanged ($\Delta{=}0$pp, n.s.).

\subsection{When all three tests agree}
\label{sec:triangulation}

Triangulation labels are calibration points, not primary results: they
validate that the three probes \emph{can} co-align on the same instance.
Under strict thresholds on 440 ALGO instances, we obtain 8
retrieval-consistent, 4 computation-consistent, 157 mixed, and 271 (61.6\%)
ambiguous; GSM (Claude, $n{=}44$): 35 computation-leaning, 9 ambiguous. Label
rates are threshold-sensitive: a liberal v2 sweep yields $\sim$58\% strong
labels vs.\ $\sim$3\% under legacy strict rules
(Table~\ref{tab:thresholds}; Appendix~\ref{app:triangulation}). The primary
findings are the behavioural patterns in
Sections~\ref{sec:inversion}--\ref{sec:bw}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The five models form qualitatively different reasoners---Claude declares
plans reliably (GSM CCI${=}0.231$) without correctness coupling; GPT-4o rarely
follows declarations (median CCI${=}0$) yet recovers; Gemini peaks on
canonicals (91\%) but format-ignores injections; Llama sits lowest overall;
\omini{} solves near-perfectly and complies with 100\% of injections.
Benchmarks are not broken; treating one accuracy number as the complete
picture is. Across 219 canonical problems (the full bank: 44 GSM, 65 BW, 110
ALGO), 6 variants, 5 models, and $\sim$20K model API calls (coverage in
Table~\ref{tab:coverage}), three independent tests reveal patterns accuracy
hides:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Matched-benchmark models differ in \wthree{} robustness (GSM
  calibration at $n{=}44$ for Claude/Gemini/\omini, partial at $n{=}20$ for
  GPT-4o/Llama), and the direction reverses across problem types.
  \item Plan fidelity does not predict correctness: GPT-4o (median CCI${=}0$)
  is correct $\sim$70\% of the time.
  \item Accepting a wrong intermediate at 88--93\% does not reliably derail
  the final answer.
  \item Same DP, different exposure: WIS collapses under \wthree{}
  universally---consistent with both lower exposure and higher baseline
  difficulty (Table~\ref{tab:proximity}); CC partially survives (\omini{}
  caveat, Section~\ref{sec:proximity}).
  \item BW rename: Claude ${+}23.9$pp, Gemini ${+}18.5$pp, Llama ${-}22.0$pp;
  GPT-4o/\omini{} unchanged.

\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Coverage and scope.}
All results are zero-shot CoT at $T{=}0$, English structured reasoning only.
GSM GPT-4o/Llama Probe-1 covers $n{=}20$ bank-valid problems
(GSM\_001--020); Wilson CIs are $\sim$30pp wide---we report these as partial
replication, not powered five-model evidence (headline inversions use ALGO
SP-adv, $n{=}34$). GSM\_041--064 gap fill, ALGO Phase-2A elicited sessions
(49/110 per model), a difficulty-matched WIS bank, and second
reasoning-trained providers (DeepSeek-R1~\citep{guo2025deepseek},
Qwen3) are queued on restored API access, not on study design.
Section~\ref{sec:proximity}'s proximity-link observation is
one-model-strong (\omini{} only). CCI comparisons across models partly
reflect Phase-1 format propensity; 17/29 GPT-4o zero-CCI sessions are
parser-empty by construction (Section~\ref{sec:metrics}). Few-shot
demonstrations are known to partially restore accuracy under template
perturbation~\citep{mirzadeh2024gsm}; we present \wthree{} numbers as
zero-shot estimates, not capability upper bounds. WIS subtype collapse is
observational: exposure and difficulty co-vary (Table~\ref{tab:proximity}).

\paragraph{Measurement limits.}
The strict BW PDDL protocol aborts at 84--100\%, yielding a protocol-level
finding rather than per-model CCI; the NL-tolerant rerun covers all five
models. CCI is a behavioural metric only---it does not probe internal
reasoning faithfulness. Infini-gram overlap is a public-corpus proximity
proxy for closed-weight models, not a confirmed contamination signal; the
within-ALGO CC$\to$SP$\to$WIS gradient is the cleaner internal control. The
full per-(probe, model) coverage matrix and extended scoping decisions
appear in Appendix~\ref{app:cov}.
