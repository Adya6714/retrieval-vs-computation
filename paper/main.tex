\begin{abstract}
Benchmarks that perturb the surface form of reasoning problems report accuracy drops and
attribute them to memorisation. We audit what those drops measure. Applying six surface
variants to 219 problems across arithmetic, planning, and algorithmic optimisation, on six
models, we find that three confounds each independently produce drop-shaped signals. First,
the answer-checker is itself a program: because obfuscation perturbs exactly the tokens a
checker parses, verifier design is correlated with the treatment rather than random, and it
biases toward the memorisation hypothesis. Holding responses fixed and removing one verifier
component moves a planning cell from $0.252$ to $0.043$ with zero movement on the unperturbed
condition. Second, canonical and regenerated instances are not difficulty-matched. On
Blocksworld, regenerated instances are harder on every structural measure we can compute,
including optimal plan length from an admissible planner, yet four of six models score
\emph{higher} on them; Claude Sonnet~4 moves from $0.170$ to $0.638$ unmatched. Stratifying on
block-naming convention shrinks that gap to within-stratum deltas of $+0.090$ and $+0.064$
(matched naming $n{=}13$: $+0.077$), so the headline inversion is confounded by lexical form as
well as difficulty. $n$-gram proximity to public corpora does not account for the residual gap.
Third, benchmark items are not independent: our algorithmic bank of 110 problems collapses to
51 clone families, so nominal intervals are $1.6$--$2.7\times$ too narrow. Beyond the confounds,
two structural results survive. Surface fragility has no item-level locus --- no problem fails
across all models in arithmetic or algorithms --- and plan-execution consistency covaries with
rename survival when pooled across families ($r{=}0.243$, $p{=}0.024$, $n{=}272$, cluster
bootstrap) while remaining distinct from raw accuracy. We release per-instance verifier logs and
a gold-in-gold-out harness that gates verifier validity before any model is queried.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A now-standard evaluation protocol takes a reasoning benchmark, perturbs the surface form of
each item while preserving its solution, and reports the accuracy drop as evidence that models
rely on memorised surface patterns rather than generalisable procedure
\citep{mirzadeh2025gsmsymbolic, valmeekam2023planbench, wang2024rupbench, huang2025mathperturb}.
The inference is attractive because the manipulation is clean: if the problem is logically
identical and only the words changed, a drop looks like direct evidence about representation.

This paper asks what else could produce that drop. We are not arguing the phenomenon is
absent. We are arguing that the standard protocol, as implemented across several widely-used
benchmarks, admits at least three alternative explanations that are rarely controlled and that
each bias in the same direction as the hypothesis under test.

\paragraph{The oracle is a program.} Automated evaluation depends on an answer-checker, and in
software testing the reliability of that checker is known as the \emph{test oracle problem}
\citep{barr2015oracle}. Obfuscation benchmarks have an unusually severe version of it. The
perturbation renames exactly the entities, actions, and identifiers that a checker must parse
to score an answer. A checker that silently fails to invert the rename returns
\texttt{incorrect} on the perturbed condition and \texttt{correct} on the canonical one, for
every model. This is not measurement noise: the failure is correlated with the treatment, so
it produces bias, and the bias points toward the memorisation hypothesis. Section~\ref{sec:oracle}
ablates three verifier components under fixed responses and shows that the measured drop is
verifier-dependent.

\paragraph{Regenerated instances are not matched on difficulty or lexical form.} Comparing a
benchmark's released items against freshly generated ones from the same domain is a natural
exposure control. It is only valid if the two sets are matched on difficulty \emph{and} surface
conventions. On Blocksworld we compute optimal plan length with an admissible planner and find
the regenerated set is harder on structural axes --- yet the unmatched accuracy inversion is
largely attenuated once block-naming convention is stratified (Section~\ref{sec:difficulty}).
Uncontrolled difficulty can manufacture a drop, uncontrolled lexical form can manufacture an
inversion, and either can hide the other.

\paragraph{Items are not independent.} Procedurally generated banks produce clone families.
Our 110-problem algorithmic bank contains 14 families covering 73 problems, giving an effective
$n$ of 51. Confidence intervals computed under an independence assumption are $1.6$--$2.7\times$
too narrow (Section~\ref{sec:clones}). We are not aware of a rename-robustness paper that
reports effective $n$.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item A difficulty- and naming-stratified comparison of benchmark-released versus procedurally
  regenerated planning instances, using admissible-planner optimal plan length as a structural
  difficulty measure and documenting that the unmatched accuracy inversion is confounded by
  block-naming convention (matched $n{=}13$).
\item A verifier-ablation characterisation of treatment-correlated scoring sensitivity in
  obfuscation designs, with a gold-in-gold-out gate and three documented component toggles
  with their magnitude and sign.
\item Evidence that surface fragility has no item-level locus and that plan-execution
  consistency shows convergent association with rename survival when pooled, while remaining
  discriminant from raw accuracy.
\item Per-instance verifier logs, exclusion codes with reasons, and clone-family annotations
  for a 219-problem bank across three domains.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Surface-form robustness.} \citet{mirzadeh2025gsmsymbolic} introduce GSM-Symbolic,
which regenerates GSM8K items with varied names and values and reports group-level accuracy
decline; their \emph{Vary Name} condition is the direct predecessor of our entity-rename
variant on arithmetic. \citet{valmeekam2023planbench} introduce PlanBench, whose Obfuscated
Mystery Blocksworld replaces domain predicates with arbitrary tokens; our planning rename
variant uses that design and, for the mystery subset, that vocabulary. \citet{wu2024reasoning}
extend counterfactual evaluation across eleven tasks, \citet{wang2024rupbench} apply nine
perturbation types across fifteen datasets, \citet{li2024gsmplus} generate GSM8K variants, and
\citet{huang2025mathperturb} construct hard perturbations on competition mathematics.
\citet{hao2025putnamgap} stress-test mathematically equivalent transformations and observe
sensitivity to variable naming and formatting. Our contribution is not a new perturbation. It
is a controlled audit of what the existing ones measure once oracle reliability, difficulty
matching, and item independence are handled.

\paragraph{Oracles and evaluation validity.} The dependence of automated testing on a fallible
oracle is well developed in software engineering \citep{barr2015oracle}. Parallel concerns
appear in NLP as benchmark-validity and construct-validity critiques
\citep{raji2021benchmark, bowman2021what}, which argue that a metric's name frequently
outruns what it measures. We instantiate that critique quantitatively for obfuscation
benchmarks and show the induced bias is directional rather than random.

\paragraph{Contamination and proximity.} \citet{razeghi2022impact} show pretraining term
frequency predicts arithmetic accuracy; \citet{golchin2024timetravel} and \citet{shi2024detecting}
develop membership-style detection. For closed-weight models no corpus ground truth exists, so
we treat Infini-gram overlap \citep{liu2024infinigram} as an explicitly labelled public-corpus
proximity proxy and use it only for within-family tests.

\paragraph{Chain-of-thought faithfulness.} \citet{turpin2023language} and
\citet{lanham2023measuring} intervene within a single reasoning trace. Our plan-execution
measure instead compares a declaration made in one session against execution in a second
session with no shared context, which isolates behavioural consistency from within-context
self-consistency.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Problem families.} We use three families chosen so that each controls a confound the
others admit. \textbf{GSM} (44 items sampled from GSM8K \citep{cobbe2021training} across
Infini-gram proximity quartiles) provides an arithmetic family with documented contamination
structure. \textbf{BW} (65 PlanBench instances \citep{valmeekam2023planbench}, 50 standard and
15 mystery) provides a planning family with a formal domain, which is what makes admissible-planner
difficulty computation possible. \textbf{ALGO} (110 hand-designed dynamic-programming problems:
coin change, shortest path, weighted interval scheduling) holds the underlying algorithm class
fixed while varying public-corpus prevalence across roughly three orders of magnitude.

\paragraph{Variants.} Each canonical item is expanded to six variants by deterministic scripts:
W1 paraphrase, W2 reformat, W3 entity rename to a vocabulary-disjoint held-out set, W4 formal
notation, W5 direction reversal, W6 procedural regeneration with the same algorithm and new
parameters. Full protocol in Appendix~\ref{app:variants}.

\paragraph{Validity gating.} Two gates run before any model is queried. \emph{Transform
validity}: a variant is retained only if it differs from its canonical in the intended way. For
W6 this means the gold answer must differ and the text must not be byte-identical; the gate
excludes 33 rows with reason \texttt{variant\_not\_transformed}, of which 25 are ALGO interval
rows whose rendered weights are unchanged under the parameter update the variant requests.
\emph{Oracle validity}: every gold answer is passed back through its own verifier
(Section~\ref{sec:oracle}). Variants failing either gate are excluded with a recorded reason
rather than scored as incorrect.

\paragraph{Models.} Six models at $T{=}0$, zero-shot chain-of-thought via a single API gateway:
Claude Sonnet~4, GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Flash, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct \citep{dubey2024llama},
o4-mini, and DeepSeek-R1-distill-Llama-70B \citep{guo2025deepseek} on the planning family.

\paragraph{Statistics.} ALGO intervals are 10{,}000-draw percentile bootstraps resampling
\emph{clone families}, not individual items (Section~\ref{sec:clones}); GSM and BW use Wilson
intervals. Retention $R_{W3}{=}\mathrm{Acc}_{W3}/\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{can}}$ is reported only
where $\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{can}}\ge 0.30$, a threshold fixed before analysis, since the ratio
is unstable near zero.

\section{Verifier Design Determines the Measured Effect}
\label{sec:oracle}

\paragraph{Why obfuscation designs are the hard case.} For a checker to score a renamed answer
it must invert the rename. If the generator stores that mapping and the checker does not consume
it, the checker cannot recognise a correct answer expressed in the renamed vocabulary. It then
returns \texttt{incorrect} for every model on the perturbed condition while leaving the canonical
condition untouched. The resulting scoring change is perfectly correlated with the experimental
treatment, so it does not average out with more items or more models. It produces a clean, large,
statistically significant drop that looks exactly like the hypothesis.

\paragraph{Gold-in-gold-out.} Before any model is queried, each item's own gold answer is
submitted to its verifier as though it were a model response. A verifier that rejects its own
gold cannot score that cell. We run this for every (family, variant) pair and treat any failure
as a blocking validity gate.

\paragraph{Verifier ablation.} Holding model responses, items, and all other pipeline settings
fixed, we toggle individual verifier components and remeasure reported accuracy
(Table~\ref{tab:oracle}). Removing the planning rename-mapping consumer drops perturbed accuracy
from $.252$ to $.043$ on $64/64$ one-directional flips, with canonical delta $.000$ --- the clean
ablation of treatment-correlated sensitivity. The graph rename-mapping and state-parser toggles
also move the perturbed condition, but they move the canonical condition as well, so each bundles
treatment-correlated sensitivity with a change in general parsing coverage; we report them as
such rather than as pure ablations of the treatment effect.

\input{tables/table_oracle}

\paragraph{Implication.} A published obfuscation result is only interpretable if the checker has
been shown to accept its own gold under the perturbed condition. We recommend reporting
gold-in-gold-out pass rates per (variant, family) cell alongside accuracy. In our pipeline this
is a single table and adds negligible runtime.

\section{Difficulty and Lexical Form Are Uncontrolled in Regenerated-Instance Comparisons}
\label{sec:difficulty}

Comparing benchmark-released items against procedurally regenerated ones is the natural exposure
control: same generator, same domain, no plausible pretraining exposure to the new items. The
comparison is valid only under difficulty matching, which is rarely checked because most domains
have no ground-truth difficulty measure. Planning does. Blocksworld has a formal PDDL encoding,
so optimal plan length is computable with an admissible planner.

\paragraph{Naming confounds the unmatched gap.}
The unmatched Claude delta of $+0.468$ is not a clean difficulty effect.
Canonical instances are overwhelmingly scattered-named ($44/47$) while regenerated instances are
overwhelmingly sequential ($37/47$). Within naming strata the Claude deltas shrink to $+0.090$
(sequential) and $+0.064$ (scattered). On the $n{=}13$ pairs matched for naming convention,
Claude moves only $+0.077$, GPT-4o $+0.154$, Gemini $-0.154$, and Llama/o4-mini/DeepSeek
$0.000$ (Table~\ref{tab:bw-stratified}). We treat the naming imbalance as an identified confound,
not as an estimated causal effect of sequential labels: the sequential-canonical cell is
$n{=}3$.

\input{tables/table_n1_stratified}

\paragraph{Goal topology is outside current covariates.}
Goal topology requires a state-parser extension we have not yet validated; we report only the
covariates we can currently compute (optimal plan length, block counts, naming convention, and
the matched-naming stratum above).

\paragraph{Contamination does not account for the residual gap.} If released instances were harder because
models have memorised them in a way that interferes, per-instance proximity should predict the
canonical-minus-regenerated gap. It does not: Spearman correlations between Infini-gram proximity
and the per-instance gap have intervals spanning zero for five of six models (Claude $-0.14$
$[-0.40, 0.11]$; GPT-4o $-0.11$; Gemini $-0.02$; Llama $-0.18$; DeepSeek $+0.21$). Only o4-mini
shows a significant negative association ($-0.44$ $[-0.60, -0.23]$, $p{=}0.001$).
Restricting to GSM W3 retention among canonically correct items
(\texttt{N5\_contamination\_vs\_retention.csv}) likewise yields null associations for every model,
with all confidence intervals spanning zero (Table~\ref{tab:n5}).

\input{tables/table_n5}

\paragraph{The effect is domain-specific.} Running the same comparison on the algorithmic family
gives the opposite result: regenerated instances are harder in accuracy for four of five models
(Claude $.667 \rightarrow .244$; o4-mini $1.000 \rightarrow .422$; Gemini $.500 \rightarrow .144$;
GPT-4o $.433 \rightarrow .200$; Llama $.033 \rightarrow .078$), and are structurally mixed rather
than uniformly harder. We therefore do not claim a general property of language models. We claim
that a widely-used planning benchmark's released instances are not interchangeable with instances
its own generator produces once difficulty \emph{and} lexical form are uncontrolled, and that any
exposure comparison using released items inherits both confounds.

\paragraph{Direction reversal (W5) raises Blocksworld accuracy.}
Appendix~\ref{app:variants} defines W5 as exchanging initial and goal states; the generator
implements exactly that swap (\texttt{swap\_pddl\_init\_goal} in
\texttt{variant\_utils.py}) and replans with Fast Downward. On the bank-restricted Probe~1 pool
this is not a drop: Claude moves $.172\rightarrow.873$, Gemini $.391\rightarrow.764$, and
\omini{} $.781\rightarrow.909$ (Table~\ref{tab:variants}). Goal/initial inversion is therefore a
positive finding about surface-form sensitivity in planning, not an unexplained table anomaly.
ALGO shortest-path W5 cells with majority \texttt{parse\_failed} exclusions
(GPT-4o $22/50$, Gemini $32/50$, \omini{} $8/50$) are reported as exclusion reasons rather than
accuracies.

\section{Fragility Has No Item-Level Locus}
\label{sec:locus}

Perturbation results are usually reported as marginal accuracies, which implicitly treats
fragility as a property of items: some problems are harder to rename-survive than others. We test
this directly by asking which items fail for \emph{every} model.

\paragraph{No shared-hard set outside obfuscated planning.} Among items where all five models
were evaluated, the number failing for all of them is $0/110$ on the algorithmic family and
$0/20$ on arithmetic. On planning it is $14/64$, and all fourteen are mystery instances. Outside
the obfuscated planning condition, essentially no item is universally fragile. Fragility is a
model--item interaction, not an item property (Figure~\ref{fig:locus}).

\paragraph{Items are not independent.}
\label{sec:clones}
Near-duplicate detection with identical gold answers collapses the 110-problem algorithmic bank
into 14 clone families covering 73 problems, for an effective $n$ of 51; the largest family has
13 members. The frozen 61-item adversarial subset collapses to 14 clusters. Re-estimating
intervals with a cluster bootstrap over families widens them by a median factor of $1.60$ on the
full bank and $1.94$ on the adversarial subset, reaching $2.66$ on individual cells. All ALGO
intervals in this paper use the cluster bootstrap. Because procedural generators produce clone
structure by construction, we expect this to generalise to other generated banks, and we
recommend reporting effective $n$ alongside nominal $n$.

\paragraph{Variant difficulty ordering is stable within family.}
Across models within a family, Kendall $W$ on the W1--W6 accuracy rank order is significant for
ALGO ($W{=}0.419$, $p{=}0.049$), BW ($W{=}0.679$, $p{<}0.001$), and GSM ($W{=}0.660$,
$p{=}0.042$) in \texttt{P1\_variant\_ordering.csv} (Table~\ref{tab:variant-order}). The same
ordering is \emph{not} stable within model across families (Claude $W{=}0.165$, $p{=}0.824$),
so ``which surface transform is hardest'' is a family property rather than a model trait.

\paragraph{Reliable crossovers exceed chance only on GSM.}
Pairwise model--item dissociation crossovers
(\texttt{C8\_dissociation\_crossovers.csv}) tested against an additive item+model null
(\texttt{C8\_null\_expectation.csv}) are at or below chance on ALGO and BW, but GSM reliable
crossovers are above chance ($20$ observed vs null mean $0.57$, $p{=}0.001$;
Table~\ref{tab:c8}). This is candidate behavioural double-dissociation evidence on arithmetic
only; it does not generalise to the other families under the same null.

\section{Convergent and Discriminant Validity of Robustness Measures}
\label{sec:construct}

If surface invariance and procedural consistency were facets of one underlying robustness
capacity, measures of each should covary (convergent validity) while remaining distinct from raw
accuracy (discriminant validity). We test this with a measure that is methodologically
independent of the rename probe: a cross-session consistency index (CCI) comparing a model's
declared solution plan, produced in one session, against its step-by-step execution in a second
session with no shared context.

\paragraph{Pooled CCI covaries with rename survival.} Pooling GSM with ALGO, the point-biserial
correlation between CCI and rename survival is $r{=}0.243$ $[0.027, 0.414]$, $p{=}0.024$,
$n{=}272$; restricted to canonically correct items it is $r{=}0.256$ $[0.043, 0.416]$,
$p{=}0.020$, $n{=}178$ (Table~\ref{tab:p2p1}). Both confidence intervals exclude zero. The
$p$-values are two-sided cluster-bootstrap probabilities from the same 5k-resample procedure that
produces the CIs (\texttt{P2\_P1\_convergence.csv}, seed~42).

\paragraph{Per-family estimates are underpowered relative to the pool.} At the GSM item level
alone the association is $0.156$ $[-0.006, 0.308]$, $p{=}0.060$, $n{=}128$
(Figure~\ref{fig:construct}); ALGO alone is $0.359$ $[-0.043, 0.664]$, $p{=}0.085$, $n{=}144$.
Across GSM models the rank correlation between mean CCI and retention is $0.600$, $p{=}0.224$,
$n{=}4$ (iid bootstrap). We read the per-family intervals that span zero as a power observation,
not as evidence against the pooled association.

(\texttt{ALGO\_P2\_cci.csv}) is Claude $0.384$ ($n{=}59$ scored), GPT-4o $0.317$ ($n{=}59$),

(Table~\ref{tab:algo-cci}).

\paragraph{Variance decomposition.} A crossed G-theory decomposition of binary accuracy
(\texttt{O10\_variance\_components.csv}, \texttt{O10\_generalizability\_coefficients.csv})
puts most ALGO primary-design variance on the model facet (proportion $0.327$) rather than
items ($0.018$), with $G_{\mathrm{item}}{=}0.415$ (Table~\ref{tab:o10}). GSM's primary design
instead shows a larger item share ($0.186$) and $G_{\mathrm{item}}{=}0.680$. This is the
variance-component backdrop for treating robustness measures as partially shared rather than
identical to item difficulty.

\input{tables/table_o10}

\paragraph{Intrusion as a second behavioural measure.} Among W3 errors, canonical-token
intrusion rates (\texttt{C1\_intrusion\_rates.csv}) reach $0.619$ for ALGO \omini{} and
$0.250$ for Claude, while BW W3 intrusion is $0$ for every model with data
(Table~\ref{tab:c1-intrusion}). Intrusion is therefore an independent behavioural signal that
does not track rename accuracy alone.

\input{tables/table_c1_intrusion}

\paragraph{Discriminant validity.} Neither robustness measure tracks accuracy. Rank correlation
between canonical accuracy and retention is $0.136$ $[-0.100, 0.800]$, $p{=}0.642$; between
canonical accuracy and the canonical--rename contingency coefficient $\phi$ it is $-0.426$
$[-0.724, 0.500]$, $p{=}0.146$. Robustness is measurably distinct from the quantity leaderboards
report. We deliberately do not present retention and $\phi$ as convergent evidence: both are
statistics of the same $2\times 2$ contingency table, so their agreement is algebraic rather than
empirical.

\paragraph{Compliance and correctness are separable.} In a mid-solve injection protocol, models
accept an injected incorrect intermediate state at high rates (Claude 88.5\%, GPT-4o 93.4\%,
o4-mini 100\%, Llama 39.3\%; Gemini treats every injection as malformed input and proceeds
without it) yet post-injection final accuracy stays near uninterrupted rates (Claude .525 vs
.541; GPT-4o .508 vs .557; o4-mini .377 vs .409). Accepting a false premise mid-derivation does
not reliably derail the final answer, so compliance is not a proxy for procedural integrity.

\section{Limits of the Present Design}
\label{sec:scope}

\subsection{Model-imposed limits}

Under a strict PDDL execution protocol, planning sessions abort on illegal actions at high
rates; routing through a natural-language tolerant parser shifts the failure mode from format
errors to precondition violations (\texttt{BW\_P2\_floor\_documentation.csv}: goal-reached rate
$0$ for Claude/GPT-4o/Llama on $n{=}50$ sessions each; mean CCI $0.013$/$0.000$/$0.109$).
Models emit genuinely illegal plans rather than mis-formatted valid ones, and BW Probe~2 CCI is
an execution floor rather than a model fingerprint. The Phase~2A (uninjected CCI) vs Phase~2B
(injected TEP) protocol split is documented in \texttt{P2\_phase2A\_2B\_protocol.csv}.

The ALGO logit-lens rank-shift vs W3-correct analysis
(\texttt{N3\_algo\_mech\_behavior\_link.csv}) is \textbf{NOT ESTIMABLE}: Llama has $1/60$
W3-correct outcomes and Qwen2.5-1.5B has $0/61$ (Table~\ref{tab:n3}). Outcome degeneracy means
the present open-weight sample cannot support a within-model correlation between representational
shift and rename survival. Separately, planning gold answers use three distinct first-action
tokens across 65 problems, so rank-based readouts over the gold token cannot discriminate
conditions in that family.

\input{tables/table_n3}

\subsection{Measurements pending inference budget}

ALGO shortest-path W5 still has majority \texttt{parse\_failed} cells for GPT-4o ($22/50$),
Gemini ($32/50$), and \omini{} ($8/50$). GSM GPT-4o and Llama Probe~1 cells are limited to
$n{=}20$ included rows per variant under \texttt{api\_error} exclusions (24 of 64 attempted,
alongside \texttt{missing\_bank\_row}). Phase-resolved execution accuracy on arithmetic is not
separable in the current logs because the implementation pools the two sessions. A
difficulty-and-naming-matched regenerated Blocksworld bank is specified and estimated at under
100 calls once the generator match criteria are locked.

\subsection{Design scope}

Closed-model corpus membership is unresolvable for Claude, GPT-4o, Gemini, \omini{}, and
DeepSeek (\texttt{O16\_CLOSED\_MODEL\_CAVEAT.md}); Infini-gram proxy scores remain available but
are not calibratable against known membership. Infini-gram windows are not cross-family
comparable ($n{\le}8$ for arithmetic, $n{\le}13$ otherwise;
\texttt{P3\_infinigram\_query\_audit.csv}). Clone families reduce effective $n$ on ALGO
(Section~\ref{sec:clones}) and all ALGO intervals use the cluster bootstrap for that reason.
Released-versus-regenerated comparisons require matching on covariates that are not obvious
\emph{a priori}; we identify two---structural difficulty and block-naming convention---and leave
goal topology outside the covariates we can currently compute
(Section~\ref{sec:difficulty}).

We fit single-class and mixture Rasch models on GSM
(\texttt{C2\_irt\_fit\_comparison.csv}); BIC selects $K{=}1$ (mixture Rasch BIC $558.1$), and
\texttt{strategy\_posteriors.csv} assigns all $72$ GSM person-rows to a single mixed class at MAP
probability $1.0$ (Table~\ref{tab:c2-irt}). ALGO and BW were not fit: O10 item variance fell
below the pre-registered gate. Per-instance triple-probe labels remain underdetermined across a
270-configuration threshold sweep (retrieval-consistent counts from 0 to 170 of 440), so we
report probe-level results rather than conjunction labels.

\section{Conclusion}

Perturbation benchmarks measure a real phenomenon, but the protocol as commonly implemented
conflates it with at least three others. Verifier design determines the measured drop under
obfuscation. Regenerated and released instances differ in difficulty \emph{and} lexical form; on
Blocksworld the unmatched accuracy inversion shrinks once naming is stratified, and goal
topology sits outside the covariates we can currently compute. Generated banks contain clone
families that halve the effective sample size. Once these are handled, fragility turns out to
have no item-level locus outside obfuscated planning, and plan-execution consistency shows
pooled convergent association with rename survival while remaining discriminant from raw accuracy
--- so reporting robustness as one number still outruns what any single cell measures.

\section{Variant Generation Protocol}
\label{app:variants}

\textbf{W1 (paraphrase).} Restated in different words; numeric values, entities, and structural
relations preserved exactly.
\textbf{W2 (format).} Prose converted to a structured representation appropriate to the family
(adjacency matrix, interval table, equation set).
\textbf{W3 (entity rename).} All entity identifiers replaced with a held-out,
vocabulary-disjoint token set, length-matched and single-token where the original was
single-token. The generator persists the mapping; the verifier consumes it
(Section~\ref{sec:oracle}).
\textbf{W4 (formal notation).} \LaTeX{} for arithmetic, formal predicates for planning,
mathematical edge-list notation for graph and interval problems.
\textbf{W5 (direction reversal).} Planning: initial and goal states exchanged. Arithmetic:
forward problems inverted to backward form. Graph: source and destination exchanged. Not defined
for coin change or interval scheduling, which have no meaningful reversal.
\textbf{W6 (procedural regeneration).} Same algorithm and parameter ranges, new numeric values.
A regenerated item is retained only if its gold answer differs from the canonical and its text is
not byte-identical.

Every variant passes its family verifier on the gold answer before any model is queried.

\section{Metric Definitions}
\label{app:metrics}

$R_{W3} = \mathrm{Acc}_{W3}/\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{can}}$, reported only where
$\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{can}} \ge 0.30$.
$\phi$ is the contingency coefficient of canonical and rename correctness over items.
CCI is the fraction of declared numeric steps matched by second-session execution within
$\epsilon{=}0.01$.
Error propagation is the fraction of post-injection steps differing from the uninjected run.
Proximity is Infini-gram $n$-gram overlap against public corpora, used only within family;
window sizes differ across families (maximum $n{=}8$ for arithmetic, $13$ for the others) and the
count is unnormalised, so cross-family comparison is not valid.

\section{Protocol and Contamination Audit Notes}
\label{app:audits}

\paragraph{Infini-gram query construction.}
\texttt{P3\_infinigram\_query\_audit.csv} records that GSM uses full problem text with
$\max n{=}8$, while ALGO/BW default to $\max n{=}13$ on index \texttt{v4\_rpj\_llama\_s4}, so
contamination scores are not comparable across families. ALGO ``template''/``instance'' columns
share the full problem text under different $n$ caps (template forced through the GSM $n{=}8$
path; instance uses the gold string), so they are not a true template/instance split. BW instance
fallback \texttt{blocksworld num\_blocks N} is a keyword dump when no goal snippet is present.

\paragraph{BW Probe~2 floor.}
\texttt{BW\_P2\_floor\_documentation.csv} documents goal-reached rate $0$ for all three models
with data and places BW CCI/TEP at an execution floor; those series are not used as model
fingerprints in the main text.

\paragraph{Phase 2A/2B separation.}
\texttt{P2\_phase2A\_2B\_protocol.csv} lists the scripts, output files, and whether injection
lands at \texttt{critical\_step\_index} for ALGO, GSM, and BW. ALGO 2A/2B are separate process
invocations; GSM stores CCI and TEP on one row; BW CCI and TEP use distinct scripts.

\paragraph{Closed-model contamination caveat.}
As stated in \texttt{O16\_CLOSED\_MODEL\_CAVEAT.md}, exact/near-exact corpus membership is
identifiable only for open-corpus models (Pythia/Pile, OLMo/Dolma). For Claude, GPT-4o, Gemini,
\omini{}, and DeepSeek the ground-truth membership cell cannot be constructed; Infini-gram proxy
scores remain available but are not calibratable against known membership.
