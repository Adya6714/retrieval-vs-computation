# BM-03 — Thinking Checklists (run these on every result and every claim)

## When a result comes back
1. What is the ALTERNATIVE explanation? (tokenization? difficulty? a parser bug? luck?)
2. What HIDDEN ASSUMPTION am I making? (that the label means what I think?)
3. What would FALSIFY this? Can I run that check cheaply?
4. Does anything in 05_Papers CONTRADICT it?
5. Is the EFFECT SIZE big enough to carry the claim, or am I over-reading noise?
6. Which reviewer attack in [[EF-06_Open_Methodological_Questions]] does this touch?
7. What HUMAN-SCIENCE construct does this map to? (see [[LB-02_Problem_Statement_Engine]])

## Before writing any claim in a draft
- Where is the exact evidence (file + row filter + script)?
- Is it statistically significant, with a confidence interval?
- Could Reviewer 2 reject this sentence? What one extra experiment kills the objection?
- Am I overstating? Replace "shows/proves" with "is consistent with" unless calibrated.
- Have I cited the nearest prior work and said exactly how I differ?

## Before starting any new experiment (pre-mortem)
- Hypothesis, and the RIVAL hypothesis that predicts different data?
- Independent variable, dependent variable, and the confound I'm controlling?
- What result would make me ABANDON the idea? (write it down now)
- Cheapest version that still tests the core claim? (do that first)

## The "is this novel enough" test (from [[LB-02_Problem_Statement_Engine]])
1. Names a CONSTRUCT, not a dataset. 2. Has a FALSIFIABLE dissociation. 3. Has a VALIDATION path (ground truth / causal / ecological). 4. Gives a PER-INSTANCE readout. 5. Names WHO acts on the answer. All five, or it's weak.
